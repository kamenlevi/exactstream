Latest results and predictions: a 125B mixture-of-experts model on a 64 GB Mac (November 2026)

Kamen Levi

Independent researcher

23 November 2026

PDF

Abstract

This report collects every result and projection on running Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, a 125B mixture-of-experts model, on a 64 GB Mac between the ExactStream 1.2.0 release (12 November 2026) and the project's pause on 19 November; nothing was re-run. No new end-to-end speed was measured, so the published 164 tok/s lossy and 101 tok/s exact on HumanEval at 45 GB stand. Measured: a lossy prompt path cut the median first token on short chat prompts from 2.44 s to 0.47 s and passed its gate; a 32-row exact check equals the same rows checked 16 at a time, bit for bit; under a fixed kernel arithmetic order a 16-token chain check equals one-token decoding on 16 of 16 prompts, while exact tree checks still flip one near-tie; close-call acceptance at 1 nat passed its screening gate but kept only 2.9-5.7 % more tokens per check (about 22 % priced) and raised runaway answers against the exact reference; expert precision tiers and 2-nat close calls failed their gates. A 32-row check costs 90-102 ms exact and 50-56 ms lossy in the lab and is GPU-bound, against a byte floor of about 58 ms exact. Projections that were never run end to end: about 213-235 tok/s lossy on HumanEval+ development prompts and 150-155 on MBPP with every measured lever combined, and about 130-160 tok/s exact; 550 tok/s would need a 28-30 ms 32-row check that does not exist yet.
