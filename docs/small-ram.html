A 125B mixture-of-experts model in 32, 24 and 12 GB of memory on one Mac

SSD-streamed experts, a verified drafting loop at 12 GB, and where small memory loses: whole decode, whole request and first token for each memory line.

Kamen Levi

Independent researcher

11 November 2026

PDF

Abstract

We run Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, a 125B mixture-of-experts model (48 layers, 512 routed experts per layer, 10 per token), on one MacBook Pro (M5 Pro, 18-core CPU, 20-core GPU, 64 GB, 2 TB SSD). Experts are streamed from the internal SSD. On our speed sets the process's own lifetime peak footprint stays under 32, 24 or 12 GB, except for one 520-token math prompt at 24 GB (24.01 GB); longer prompts go over the 24 and 32 GB lines (section 6.3).

At 12 GB a block drafter's 15 drafts are checked by the 12 GB model in calls of 2-4 rows. Experts are stored at about 3.2 bits per weight.

  • The loop is verified: every committed token is the model's greedy argmax at its computed width. It is not bit-identical to one-token decoding.
  • It is deterministic and independent of the cache contents and of the prompt order.
  • On 12 HumanEval + 12 MBPP it runs 18.3 tok/s whole decode, 13.1 tok/s whole request and 4.3 s first token, at an 11.14 GB peak. On chat (12 IFEval) it runs 12.8 / 10.1 tok/s and 2.9 s, at 10.96 GB [K].

At 24 GB a lossy configuration ([MAIN]'s L + COH with 141 + 26 expert slots per layer and checks of at most 13 rows) runs 130.3 tok/s whole decode, 40.8 tok/s whole request and 3.54 s first token on 12 HumanEval, at a 23.82 GB peak [K].

  • It passed its quality gate: the same scores as the exact reference on IFBench, GPQA Diamond and LiveCodeBench v6, in both orders [K].
  • In our runs it stayed under 24 GB on prompts of up to about 500 tokens, with little room: a 448-token prompt reached 23.954 GB and a 520-token one 24.01 GB. Longer prompts use more: about 25.0 GB at 3K tokens, 25.8 GB at 10K and 28.2 GB at 30K [K].
  • On 12 MBPP it runs 91.2 / 26.1 tok/s and 3.3 s, on chat (12 IFEval) 30.6 / 18.1 tok/s and 3.1 s, and on 12 math prompts 40.8 / 27.4 tok/s and 2.9 s [K].
  • An older exact configuration (exact to its own stock-kernel reference) runs 41.1 tok/s whole decode on 12 HumanEval and 37.4 on 12 MBPP, at a 23.86 GB peak [K]. Its whole request and first token were not recorded.

At 32 GB, on chat (12 IFEval) [K]:

  • The exact configuration, token-identical to the 45 GB reference on 12 / 12 prompts, runs 33.8 tok/s whole decode, 19.8 whole request and 2.8 s first token, at a 30.62 GB peak.
  • A lossy chat configuration, not gated at 32 GB, runs 51.3 / 22.5 / 3.0 s at 31.22 GB.

Where we lose.

  • Strata (MIT; github.com/Niko1221/Strata), an open-source engine for this model, cannot run at 12 GB: its smallest setting peaks at 14.19 GB.
  • But at that setting its chat beats ours: 14.4 vs 12.8 tok/s decode, 11.2 vs 10.1 whole request, 2.8 vs 2.9 s first token.
  • At 24 GB, on the same prompts, its first token (1.3-2.3 s) is ahead of ours (2.9-3.5 s). On chat its whole request is also ahead (20.3-21.0 vs 18.1 tok/s), and our decode leads only 1.1x. On code and math our whole decode (40.8-130.3 vs 26.1-28.0 tok/s) and whole request (26.1-40.8 vs 20.3-23.0) lead.
  • Our 24 GB configuration can go over 24 GB on prompts longer than about 500 tokens: 25.0 GB at 3K tokens, 28.2 GB at 30K. On six long gate prompts (726-2,801 tokens), 256-token prompt-pass chunks kept it under, but cost 2.0-2.9x the first token and changed the tokens, so its gate does not cover them.
  • Our 12 GB first token is 2.9-4.3 s, because the prompt pass streams experts from the SSD: about 22 GB for a 63-token prompt.
  • Our 12 GB lossy two-pass mode failed its HumanEval+ gate at every threshold tried.

Two early headlines were wrong. "12 GB, 40.8 tok/s exact" was a lossy arm, and "the likely first at <= 12 GB, exact" falls with it.

We measure where a 12 GB verify call spends its time. Levers priced and not yet run:

  • two-row verify calls on chat [P: +10-15 %]
  • Strata's next-layer read-ahead
  • a lossy 24 GB chat route that reads 81.7 MB of SSD per kept token instead of 210.2 [P]
