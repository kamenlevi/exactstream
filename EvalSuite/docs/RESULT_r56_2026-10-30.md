## What the FAIL is: the live loop leaves REF2's AR once the AR passes 512 keys
A diagnostic process (`evalsuite/live16diag.py`, adfd491; disclosed, not a verdict row) re-ran each differing prompt.
It ran REF2's AR the way the reference generator runs it (`ref2.prefill` + `ar_step`, PF1 on), then the live loop,
comparing every kept check row with the AR row at the same position, bit for bit.
- **The reference didn't move.** Today's REF2 AR reproduces the stored `results/ref2` row token for token, up to and
  including the differing token, on all 8 prompts.
- **The loop does move, at one place.** Every compared row before it is bitwise equal to the AR. On 7 of 8 prompts the
  first row that differs is at input position 497 (498 on one). REF2's AR pads attention to 16 rows (N = p + 16),
  so that is where the AR first has more than 512 keys (513–514), while the check holding that row still has 502–512.
  - geometry/702's prompt is 520 tokens, so both sides are past 512 from the start. It differs from its second
    check's row 1 on (keys 538 vs 537); only 2 of 118 rows are equal.
  - Each first differing row has ~209k–241k of its 248k logits different, by up to 0.125–1.67.
- **Leap's STEP 4a (02:3x, `~/Leap`) puts it in the QSA attention call.** Past ~512 keys, the bits of MLX's full NAX
  attention kernel depend on the key length. At 383 keys (HumanEval/68), all 16 check rows are exact.
- **The tokens change only at ties.** At every differing token the AR's top two are tied or one bf16 step apart, and
  the loop's row, a step off, orders them the other way:
