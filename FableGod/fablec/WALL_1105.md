Every form that passes sits at x0.8 or above with <= 1 point of non-tie flips; x0.67-0.69 costs 3-4 points; x0.5 costs 8:
about 8x anything that has passed and 2-3x what failed. The reasons are structural:
1. **Rows.** A chat check verifies 3-3.7 rows for 1.9-2.4 kept; the perfect stop is x0.72-0.74. Plain one-token decoding
   would compute 480 tiles per token against the live check's 490 per kept token [K cloud3 P2]: on chat, speculation saves
   the dense pass, not the experts.
2. **Sure rows.** 20-23 % of chat rows have cf >= 0.99 (42-45 % >= 0.9); the 125B's own margin calls 32-40 % sure; both

3. **Irreducibility** (this run). A dropped expert is an orthogonal vector lost; no neighbour, previous row, linear map or
   mean brings it back.
4. **Convexity.** The mid rows (52-60 % of chat rows) tolerate a total per-layer error near 0.15-0.2 (top-8's) and not 0.3:
   that is one or two ranks, or half the hidden units of the tail experts, never half the experts.

**>= 2x on chat needs one of these to break:**
- A1, the bar. Not the tie rows: the non-tie read removes them and the conclusion stands. Task scores agree where they
  exist (router-mass budgets fail IFEval by 5-7 points [K QUEUE M-09]; frugal top-2 -6.0 [K TRIED 163]; top-8 passes 5/5).
- A2, irreducibility: experts trained to share structure; needs training.
- A3, the fixed cost per group: thin tails. With today's selectors the same error buys x0.72 instead of top-8's x0.82;
  an oracle selector x0.47 (m 160) and, stacked with one more small cut, about 2x. The missing piece is a predictor of an
  expert's active units that costs <= 10 % of the expert and keeps >= 0.9 of the energy at a quarter of the units (the

- A4, the rows: a chat drafter keeping >= 5 per check cuts tiles per kept token by the kept factor and touches no quality

**4b. A crude CPU predictor of map flips from masks (`wall_flipmodel2.py` -> `.out`), and its limits.** Since expert outputs
are orthogonal with near-equal norms, a mask's relative error on a row-layer follows from the kept ranks alone (eps^2 = the
dropped ranks' share of the squared weights). Model: a row flips when the logit noise exceeds its baseline margin m0,
P = Phi(-m0 / sqrt(a_e De + a_m Dm + b_e Ce + b_m Cm + s0)), with D the row's own mean eps^2 in the end layers (0-7, 36-47)
and the middle layers, C the decayed mean of the earlier rows' damage. Fit on all rows of seven rule-based strict arms: it
reproduces the five gate arms within 10-30 % leave-one-arm-out (e.g. gate_top0_cf0999_L08_35 non-tie 0.63 / 2.25 / 1.33
measured, 0.75 / 2.26 / 1.58 predicted) but compresses the uniform arms and runs 2-3x high on the forms it never saw
(twin 0.21 measured vs 0.67; split 0.99 0.51 vs 1.10; split 0.9 0.78 vs 1.58; the chat route 0.93 / 0.85 vs 4.95 / 3.26):
resident-keep damage is smaller than any training arm's. Use it for ORDER only, scaled to the measured split arms; its
order on code (non-tie): b2c < b2d < A < A' < B = split 0.9 + reach 0.7. Refit on thirteen arms (`wall_flipmodel3.out`:
the seven plus six replayed resident-keep / split / member forms), its leave-one-arm-out ratio to the measured non-tie
flips is median 1.38, 10-90 % 0.69-2.44, a geometric scatter of x1.64 over 33 arm-domains. A better predictor needs arms
that separate end-layer from middle-layer damage and own from context damage; Friday's K / A / A' / B arms are such arms.
