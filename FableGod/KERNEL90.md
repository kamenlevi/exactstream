## 2. The answer to Kamen's question, plainly
**Q: the best kernels reach 80-90 % of bandwidth; why can't ours, and why can't we use their principles if they already exist for the Mac (even though MLX's own kernel is only 95 GB/s)?**

### 2.1 Because our shape sits at the machine's balance point, and theirs sits 5-20x below it [P on K]
A kernel streams at 90 % only if everything else it does fits inside the stream time with room to spare. Per byte streamed, this M5 Pro affords:
| resource | rate [K] | per byte at 258-287 GB/s [P] |
|---|---|---|
| matrix unit, bf16 x uint4 from threadgroup memory (NAX6's operand form) | 12.4 T MAC/s (0.755 G op/s of 16x32x32) [K N7-4, M27-C] | 43-48 MACs |
| matrix unit, bf16 x bf16 in registers (peak measured) | 14.0 T MAC/s [K M27-P] | 49-54 MACs |
| matrix unit, int8 x int4 from threadgroup memory | 19.2 T MAC/s (1.169 G op/s) [K M27-C] | 67-74 MACs |
| SIMD ALUs, fp32 FMA, 20 cores (Apple publishes no number) | ~4.1 T lane-ops/s [G: 20 x 128 lanes x ~1.6 GHz] | ~14 ALU ops |

What one expert record costs: 4.92 M weights in 1.98 MB per (expert, tier) group at S 16 (MSB-3 codes + scales / biases, LSB-1 sidecar on ~30 % of experts) [K mission 27 section 1], i.e. 0.40 B per weight.
- **Our tensor path:** the smallest op shape that builds is 16 rows (16x32x16 / 16x32x32; 16x16x16 does not build [K M27-P]). Every weight is multiplied 16 times whether 1 or 16 rows are real (2.1 are, at S 16 [K routing]). That is 16 / 0.40 = **39.7 MACs per byte against 43-54 available: the op set is 0.72-0.91 of the stream time.** For 90 % of the stream, the matrix unit must be 65-82 % busy at the same moment as DRAM is 90 % busy, with a cooperative op that blocks its simdgroup while it runs [K N7-2]. No GEMM reaches 90 % of both of its roofline edges at once; 75-85 % of each is what good kernels do in the balanced corner.
- **The ALU path (what MLX's qmv, llama.cpp's mul_mv and 3c do):** a 3-bit weight costs ~1.25 integer ops to decode and ~2-3 ops per real row (unpack-select, convert, FMA) [K nax_tput mode 1; K-61 / D3C]. At 1 row that is ~3.5-4.3 ops per weight = 9-11 per byte, 60-75 % of the ALU budget: byte-bound, which is why MLX's qmv streams at 70-80 % at 1-4 rows (Rnd26: 204-239 GB/s [K moonshots]). At our 2.1 real rows per expert it is 5.5-7.6 ops per weight = 14-19 per byte: **100-130 % of the budget: ALU-bound**, which is why 3c holds 146 GB/s (51 %) and runs only 8.9 % faster with no DRAM at all [K D3C], and why MLX's gather_qmm at 16 rows (per-row passes, ~16x the per-row work) sits near 95 GB/s.
- **The kernels Kamen means** (MLX qmv / qmm at 1-8 rows, llama.cpp mul_mv, MPS GEMV, metal-flash-attention): one or a few rows per streamed weight, 2-4 ALU ops per weight, no tensor unit, a 2-5x compute margin per byte. **On an H200 the same 16-row tensor tile over fp8 weights needs 16 MACs per byte against ~206 available (990 T MAC/s / 4.8 TB/s) [P on public specs]: a 13x margin.** MonoMoE's persistent weight-major kernel and the DeepGEMM / FlashInfer decode kernels are byte-bound by construction there. The same designs here hit the matrix unit's wall: that is the whole difference, in one number (~1x vs ~13x).

So the honest statement: **the exact 16-row tensor-op family on this chip has a practical ceiling around 85 %, set by the ratio above, not by its code**; the inner loop has been tried every way (section 2.4). 90 %+ needs compute margin: int8 operands (1.55x [K M27-C]) and taking the singleton groups off the padded tile (the hybrid), or both (NAX-CH, section 3).
