## 0. The structural fact that decides Job 1: rows inside a check cannot draft, they can only verify
A check is layer-major: every row's input token must be known at layer 1, and a row's prediction for the next position exists only after layer 48. So "the rows that draft the next block inside a check" can only run a chain someone else drafted (DFlash2's) through a cheap model. Their outputs are right only up to DFlash2's first miss plus one; after it they are conditioned on the wrong token (the off-by-one case). Your own measurements of that mechanism: M-06 RECYCLE with the EXACT rows after the miss gave +0.02 (code) / +0.06 (math) kept per check; M-16's runahead model keeps tokens per pass unchanged (code 10.54 -> 10.54, chat 2.03 -> 2.03 uncapped) and gains only from pipelining and SSD lead. A near-copy's rows cannot do better than the exact rows' outputs did in M-06. Iterating (Jacobi) advances the correct prefix by ~1-2 positions per pass.
The D-01 near-copy numbers (13.4 kept on code, 8.0 on chat) are teacher-forced along the true prefix, i.e. what the near-copy keeps when run AUTOREGRESSIVELY, one step per token. So a self-drafter that inherits them must be sequential: S steps of a full 48-layer forward at M = 1 per block, each step reading the dense trunk. That is the cost that matters, not M-16's beta per row. Both forms are costed below.

# MISSION 5 (21:03 EET) — real thinking: what a token requires, the assumption to break, the equivalent move, and three directions
Budget: account 5h meter 39% at the start of this write (other sessions share it); rule: stop at 51%, end by 55%. STOP absent. ## 1. What one token of this model really requires, and where the waste is
Information. The greedy token is a deterministic function of the context, so its information content is ~0 bits; what costs is the computation that resolves it. Measured difficulty [K]: on code the 125B is >= 0.99 sure on 89 % of positions, on math 80 %, on IFEval 34 %, on chat 39 %; near-ties (p1 - p2 <= 0.1) are 1-8 % of positions. A 0.47B drafter resolves the sure positions at 0.996 (code) with ~0.1 GFLOP and ~20 MB of weights per token. So on code, ~90 % of positions need almost no computation to decide, and the remaining ~10 % need the 125B.
Computation spent today [K]: one full 125B forward per emitted token, batched 16 rows at a time: 4.8 GFLOP and 0.85 GB of expert bytes + 0.2 GB of dense bytes per kept token (4.5 GB at M = 1). Every method in the field (ours, vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp, MLX, EAGLE, Medusa, DFlash) spends this on every position, sure or not, because verification is defined as recomputing the full model at every position. Speculative decoding did not reduce the per-token computation; it only amortised the weight READS across positions (bytes 4.5 -> 0.85 GB per token), and in a 512-expert MoE even that amortisation is weak (the expert union grows as N^0.8).
Idea-level waste, ranked: (a) on code ~89 % of the verification bytes go to positions whose answer a 20 MB model already knew at 0.996; (b) the drafter and the verifier do not share computation: the trunk computed by the check at the prefix is exactly what the drafter needs for the next positions, and the drafter only sees 5 tap vectors of it; (c) the model's own argmax is only reproducible to 1-5 % (float-order floor 15.2 / 13.7 of 16; the int4 twin flips 1 % of code and 5 % of chat tokens with identical scores), yet "exact" is enforced at 100 %. The theoretical floor under "exact at every token" is one batched forward per token; nothing in the field goes below it. The 10x is only available below it.

## 2. The assumption most likely wrong
Named precisely: "An emitted token is correct only if the full model has been evaluated at that position." Everyone enforces it, including us ("bit-identical to the model's own one-token decode"). Why it is probably wrong: (i) the model's own output at mid-confidence positions is numerical noise at the 1-5 % level [K], so token-level fidelity to one computation is stricter than the model's own reproducibility; (ii) the cost is spent uniformly while the information is concentrated on ~10 % (code) to ~60 % (chat) of positions [K]; (iii) the conservative form of the alternative has already been measured as score-neutral on this model: PMIN12 (p_min 0.5 on the 12 GB chain) FAILED only its memory line, "scores identical" [K, tonight's EvalSuite note]; the Oct 21 BiLD-style test that failed (KLD 3x) skipped 24 % of positions including mid tokens, a far more aggressive operating point [K].
The precise replacement: a token is correct if either the full model produced it, or a drafter produced it with confidence >= 0.99 AND the model's state is advanced through it exactly before the next uncertain position. Under this rule the model's KV / GDN state is never fake (the "faked token stays in the state" failure mode is removed by construction); the only deviation is ~0.3-0.4 % of code tokens (the drafter's confident misses) [P: D-14 A gives 0.996 depth-1 on 125B-sure tokens; the drafter-confidence-conditioned number is in D-23's logs and must be read].
Second candidate, the one that stays inside exactness: "a drafter is a small model distilled from the target." The near-copy data [K] says the opposite: the 125B's trunk with 10 % of its experts agrees 0.98 / 0.90 (code / chat); a 5-layer student with all the prefix features agrees 0.93 / 0.66; the native 2.6B one-layer head with the real readout and the makers' data agrees 0.64 on chat. The model's choices live in its 48-layer dense computation over the context, not in its expert knowledge. Drafters should be the trunk made cheap, not students made big.

## 3. Fields that broke similar walls, and the equivalent move here
- CPU microarchitecture: speculative execution is already ours; the move that made it pay was CONFIDENCE-GATED speculation and predictors at 99 %+ from long histories. Equivalent: commit the predictor's output where it is >= 0.99 sure (section 4, D1) and build the predictor from the machine's own pipeline (the trunk, D2).
- Video codecs: skip blocks. Blocks the predictor gets right are never re-encoded; only residuals are coded. Equivalent: sure tokens are "skip blocks"; the full model codes only residual positions (D1). Codecs are lossy by design and nobody calls them broken; the metric is PSNR at a bit rate, i.e. a quality gate, which we already have (same-scores).
- Databases: approximate query processing with error bars, and materialised views. Equivalent: D1 with its measured 0.3 % token error; materialised views = the trunk's computation reused for drafting (D2), which the field throws away after each check.
- Compression / PCP: the information is in the disagreements; verification is a checksum. A checksum over every token is what we do; a cheaper checksum that is only probabilistic is D1; a provable cheaper checksum does not exist for a 48-layer net (L1-33: bounds exclude nothing) [K].
- Neuroscience: predictive coding computes prediction ERRORS; the cortex does not run a bigger cortex to verify every word. Equivalent: D1 again, which is why I rank it first despite the policy.

## 3. Exact reuse between consecutive tokens (P-frames)
Measured redundancy [K, bytes_study]: a second row adds 0.89 GB to a 1.40 GB row, so consecutive tokens share 36 % of their expert slots; 16 rows touch 71.6 distinct experts per layer of 160 slots, so 55 % of slots within a check are repeats; the union grows as N^0.8. Hidden-state similarity between consecutive positions is not in the files I have (the dfl2d taps could give it; a CPU cosine over 5 x 2560 per token, one hour, left for MTP Head).
What exact reuse already exists: the GDN state and the attention KV ARE the P-frame of the context, recomputed for no token; the expert cache (LRU) is temporal reuse across checks; batching rows is spatial reuse within a check. What is left: none at the exact level. The per-token cost in this regime is the weight read, and a weight read is shared only by rows computed in the same pass; the previous token's expert outputs cannot be reused because the inputs differ, and a delta formulation W(x_t - x_{t-1}) reads the same weights. The on-chip memory (~50 MB) holds ~16 experts, so cross-check reuse without re-reading is impossible. The field is not ignoring an exact P-frame; the lossy P-frame (skip blocks) is D1-frugal, and the only reuse that lowers reads beyond batching is a bigger cache or the frugal state. Kill test: none needed; it is arithmetic on bandwidth-bound reads. The one measurement worth one hour: the tap cosine between consecutive positions, which bounds how much a D2 drafter can be seeded from the previous state (D3), not an exact reuse.

## 2. The deepest mismatch, from our data
Named: fine-grained MoE routing (512 experts, top-10, a router per layer) assumes every expert is resident next to the compute and dispatch is free, so quality per training FLOP can be bought by spreading knowledge across 24,576 small experts. At batch 1 on unified memory with an SSD, the cost is not computing that knowledge but MOVING it, and most of what is moved does not change the token:
- the model's choices live in the dense trunk: the trunk with the hottest 10 % of experts agrees 0.983 code / 0.90 chat [K, D-01]; ranks 0-1 at 4-bit are load-bearing while ranks 2-9 at 3-bit pass the same-scores gate [K, REF2-T]; dropping to top-8 costs only 1.24-1.45x the KLD gate [K, M-09d];
- yet the expert traffic is 0.5 GB per row at 16 rows and 128 MB of SSD per kept token on code, 60 % of the code check [K], and it amortises badly (N^0.8) and runs on an ALU-bound kernel [K];
- the dense trunk that carries the decisions is only 3.3 GB per pass (11 ms at the floor), a fifth of a floor check [K].
So the model spends ~75 % of its batch-1 bytes on expert knowledge whose lower ranks rarely decide the argmax, and ~25 % on the computation that does. The datacenter never notices because all experts sit in HBM and dispatch is an all-to-all; here every one of those bytes crosses the SSD or the 300 GB/s bus for one row. The map's top-2 / top-5 flip rates by class are the direct test (section 4).

## 3. The redesign, and what can be added at inference
What the designers would have built for this Mac at batch 1 [P]: 32-64 experts per layer, top-2, ~40 MB each, so the union saturates at 16 rows, SSD reads are long and sequential, and each expert serves >= 8 rows per check (matrix units busy); one router per group of 4 layers, or routing decided from the previous layer's output, so the expert set is known a layer group ahead and prefetch has real lead; a plain residual stream or quantisation-aware mixers (no bf16 HC); 24 wider layers instead of 48 (half the sequential depth and kernel count at the same FLOPs); a mid-depth readout trained in (LayerSkip-style) so an exact early exit exists; a drafter head trained to chain on the trunk's multi-depth features; a router regularised for temporal stickiness (consecutive tokens share 36 % of experts today; a trained prior could double that at no quality cost) [G]; a 64K vocabulary for the head.
What can be added now, with the prize in tok/s:
- Low-rank experts dropped on drafter-sure rows inside the check, within the twin's noise (quality-defined): if the map shows top-2 flips <= 1 % on code-sure positions, expert bytes on those rows fall 80 %; code at the engine floor 44 -> ~30 ms per check, ~260 -> ~360 tok/s [P, conditional on the map]; today the kernel is ALU-bound so the gain is in pairs, not bytes: 3c at top-2 on sure rows, ~-1.5 ms per row, code ~+15 % [P]. TRIED: M-09 (top-8 everywhere, KILL by whole-model KLD), fewer experts per verify row (KILL, strict gate): both served every position; this applies only where the drafter is sure and is judged by flips against the twin's accepted noise. Not tried.
- HC8 at 45 GB: -0.63 GB per pass, floor check -5 %, today -1.5 % [P; K at 12 GB, lossless].
- MTP experts resident + D-22 head: chat drafter 2.5 -> ~1 ms per step: chat +5-8 % [P].
- All-layer routing predictor (DLOG): +10 % chat / math today, 0 at the floor [G].
- Decision-layer readouts for drafting: unknown until the map [G].
- Re-scheduling (32-row passes, one-snapshot GDN rollback, indexer skip): engine items already in the leads; +5-10 % together [P].
- Expert merging for drafting: closed by orthogonality (TRIED:637-639) [K]; distilling a coarse-expert student per layer is possible but D2 (the trunk with the hot experts) is the same cost and needs no training.

## 4. Prediction for Monday's map, and what would prove the thesis wrong
If the thesis is right [P]: top-2 flips <= 1 % on code-sure and <= 3 % on code-mid, chat-sure <= 2 %, chat-mid 5-10 %; top-5 flips <= 0.3 % code, <= 2 % chat (ranks 6-10 almost never decide); moe_reuse_prev flips concentrate in the late groups (layers 24-47) at mid positions, while early-group reuse hurts broadly (M-16 step 1's layer-2 effect); hc_static flips 3-8 % (HC4 flipped 6 % from precision alone); gdn_reuse_prev > att_reuse_prev on code, the reverse on chat [G]; ctx2048 flips < 1 % code, 2-4 % chat [G].
Wrong if: top-2 flips > 5 % on code-sure positions (the low ranks DO decide, the expert traffic is not waste, and the drop-on-sure-rows route is dead), or if flips are flat across layers and parts (no concentrated decision subset: the model needs all of its computation at every token, and the only levers left are bytes and the drafter).

## 5. Does the mismatch hold for other MoE models?
Qwen3-Next (512 experts, top-10 + shared, GDN 3:1, 48 layers): the same family as this model, the mismatch transfers one to one [P]. DeepSeek-V3 / V3.2 (256 experts, top-8, shared, per-layer routers, 61 layers, MTP block with its own MoE): the same structure with 22 MB expert records; batching amortisation and the rank split should look alike, SSD reads are 8x longer so latency matters less and bytes more [P]. GPT-OSS-120B (128 experts, top-4, 36 layers, MXFP4): coarser, so batching saturates faster and records are 13 MB; the rank-vs-decision split is weaker with only four ranks [G]. Mixtral (8 experts, top-2): the opposite regime, no fine-grained waste, but 2x the active bytes per token; the mismatch there is different (too much active knowledge per token, not misplaced knowledge) [P]. So the exploit (hot-subset drafting, low-rank experts dropped on sure rows, trunk-as-drafter) is a general result for the DeepSeek / Qwen fine-grained family, which is most of the open-weight frontier today, and the decision-map scripts would transfer with new patch points.
