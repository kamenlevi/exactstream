## 1. The cost model used for "what it buys" [P on mission section 3.3-3.4]
- 45 GB FAST, code, today: one check ~90 ms at 10.5 kept = 117 tok/s. Terms that scale with the distinct (expert, layer) count r (ratio vs today): experts ~40 ms (tiles) and SSD wait ~24 ms (misses; above the ~12 ms floor the wait is 0.155 ms per miss [K FAR.md:310]). Fixed: dense 12 + drafter 5.4 + commit 5 + glue ~4 = ~26 ms. So t(r) = 26 + 64 r ms and tok/s = 10.5 / t: r 1.0 -> 117; 0.8 -> 136; 0.7 -> 148; 0.6 -> 164; 0.5 -> 181; 0.4 -> 204; 0.3 -> 233. (The 300 goal needs t = 35 ms, i.e. the fixed part alone exceeds it: expert cuts cannot reach 300 without the dense / drafter work; they reach ~230 at r 0.3.)
- 24 GB FAST, code: ~19.5 MB of SSD per kept token today [K mission 3.4] with the QOM stack; the strict (no-resident-keep) ratios below scale the MISSES; the QOM-form byte numbers are BYTES10's replay (section 2 of that file), quoted where they exist. Chat: ~88 MB today (the only passing route).
- IMPORTANT for wall (a): at 45 GB a RESIDENT expert that a row keeps still costs a tile. QOM keeps every resident rank 2-9 of a sure row (`Breakthrough/keepset/qom_keep.py` step 3), so QOM cuts bytes, not tiles: at ~75-80 % residency its distinct-computed count is close to exact. The tile lever is the STRICT form (sure rows top-k0 + union members, resident or not), i.e. Q1-UNION, whose map results are PASS on code at cf 0.9 and BETWEEN on chat (TRIED UNION-STACK, LIVE-CUT AUDIT). The numbers in section 2 are strict-form distinct counts; they are the 45 GB tile numbers and the 24 / 12 GB upper bounds.

Readings [P]:
1. On code the 30 % target is met by the published-or-adapted gate at any realizable threshold: cf >= 0.9 gives x0.51 of the distinct count (PASS on the map in the Q1-UNION form); cf >= 0.5 x0.38 (QOM's PASS level at cf 0.5 is for the resident-keep form; the strict form at 0.5 is untested on the map).
2. On chat the sureness gate alone is weak (x0.81-0.93 at cf 0.9; x0.60-0.68 at 0.5) because the chat check is 3-4 rows and the bonus row is 1 of them: gating the bonus row is worth more than every other lever combined there (x0.52 -> x0.23 at cf 0; QOM-BQ is PASS code / BETWEEN chat in the resident-keep form).
3. The rejected rows are worth x0.86 on code and x0.72-0.74 on chat at the live cut, an upper bound for every reach / stop mechanism; the reach signal alone (the product of the drafts' cf) recovers x0.93 / x0.83 / x0.86 of it on its own, and x0.88 of the Q1 0.9 count when stacked (REACH-CUT, PASS code only).
4. Per-layer k on the unsure rows in layers 8-35 (the band DM P-frame found tolerant) is worth a further x0.86 (code) to x0.75 (chat) on top of Q1 0.5; it is unmeasured on the map. A uniform static k 5 in 8-35 on every row is x0.72-0.75 alone.
5. Uniform top-8 is x0.82 everywhere and passed the Eval suite at 12 GB (TK1 5/5): the cheapest legal chat lever by the FAST rule, though M-09d's KLD says it is over the strict gate.

Reading [P/G]: by gate weight alone, swapping singletons into the union looks cheap (x0.75 at a displaced mass of 0.6 % of a row's top-10 mass), which is Lynx's argument. But a swapped-in expert is a different, orthogonal function (|cos| 0.024 here), so the output error of a swap is ~ the dropped expert's whole contribution plus the added one's, i.e. at least the DROP column's error at the same union ratio: DROP reaches x0.70 only at wmax 0.01 (6 % of a row's top-10 mass per row, on average 2 of its 10 experts) and x0.54 at 13 %. Compare: Q1 drops 8 of 10 experts (~54 % of the mass) on sure rows at 0.21 % flips, so the "mass lost" metric is a poor quality proxy; the map is the judge. What the table does say: the singleton population is where the union lives, and any realizable cut of it must be keyed on the ROW (sureness / reach / layer band), not on the expert's weight, because low-weight singletons are spread over every row, mid rows included. The union growth per row (~2.4-2.8 new experts per added row [K M-10]) is this singleton tail; only a mechanism that removes singletons on unsure rows changes the scaling, and the only measured-safe one is the per-layer band cut (lever 3 of section 4).

## 6. Leads for P3, and what the literature does not have
- Published, with overlooked potential for this setting: (a) per-layer k on unsure rows keyed on the drafter's cf (LExI / Yang / HookMoE x Q1): the one unmeasured combination with a >= 10 % cut on code and ~25 % on chat beyond the current stack; (b) ACE's score-shape slot skipping on mid rows (a second signal for the rows the cf gate cannot touch); (c) Don't-skip's uniform top-8 for chat (cheap, half-certified).
- New here, nobody published: the rejected-row share of the union at live length (x0.86 / 0.72 / 0.74) and at 16 rows (x0.651 / 0.273 / 0.251 of the union is pulled by rows past the kept ones); the gate-weight anatomy of singletons (section 5); the fact that QOM saves bytes but not tiles at 45 GB (section 1), which re-opens wall (a) for the strict forms.
- Not in the literature: any mechanism that makes rows of ONE chain share experts without substitution; every batch-aware paper gets its sharing from different requests (OEA, Lynx, SERE, ExpertFlow) or from training (ReMoE, Temporal MoE, DeaMoE). P2 measures how much sharing one chain has and where (depth, layer band, rank), to see whether anything structural is left.
- Missing data for P1's open rows: router scores for the 36 windows (the harness has them live; the export has ids only), the drafter's top-2 candidates per depth with their routes (EcoSpec row), and the engine's expert-group count per call under QOM (to confirm section 1's tile claim).
