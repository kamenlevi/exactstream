# Expert merging: MC-SMoE (arXiv 2310.01334, Li et al., ICLR 2024) and HC-SMoE (arXiv 2410.08589, Chen et al.), with REAP (2510.13999, ICLR 2026) as the counter-evidence
- Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01334 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08589 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13999. Source: WebSearch extracts [S].
- What: MC-SMoE merges experts by routing statistics then compresses the merged experts (low-rank + structured sparsity): up to -80 % memory, -20 % FLOPs with "little" loss [S]. HC-SMoE: retraining-free hierarchical clustering of experts by their OUTPUTS, merged with frequency weighting; evaluated at 25 % and 50 % expert removal on 8 zero-shot tasks. REAP argues pruning beats merging on GENERATIVE tasks (code), merging looks better only on multiple-choice.
- Maps to us: merging reduces the number of distinct experts by construction (fewer experts exist), so every check's union shrinks ~proportionally, but it is a permanent model change: Kamen's rule is all 512 experts stay (TRIED REAP / PRN / PRUNE26: KILL on policy; REAP-288 HumanEval 93.9 -> 91.5 on this model). HC-SMoE's own evidence is on 8x7B-class models with redundant experts; this model's experts are orthogonal (|cos| 0.024 best pair), so clustering would merge unrelated experts.
- TRIED status: KILL (policy + measured redundancy).
- Open: none under the rule. If the rule ever changes, the 24 / 12 GB lines are where merging would pay (fewer distinct experts = fewer bytes), not the 45 GB kernel (still 16-row tiles).
