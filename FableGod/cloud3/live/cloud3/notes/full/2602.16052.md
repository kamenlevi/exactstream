## 2. Mechanism (training-free, verification-time, one shortlist per MoE layer per draft tree)
- Router probabilities g_i(h) = softmax over all N experts (Eq. 1, L119-122). Router-based ranking (default): s_i = sum over the M draft-tree tokens of g_i(h_t); shortlist S = top-B experts by s_i (Eq. 5, L170-175; Alg. 1, L704-729). Per layer: "regardless of tree size, we load at most B experts per layer" (L107-108). No calibration, no training; ranking overhead "2–3% of total generation time" (L429-434).
- Alternatives: Static = top-B by calibration selection counts c_i (Eq. 4, L154-162), zero runtime cost, allows permanent pruning, fails at B<=16; Oracle = greedy pick minimising squared reconstruction error vs the unpruned output (Eq. 6, L186-198), needs all experts, upper bound only.
- Truncation (Eq. 7, L209-225): token keeps its natural top-k; experts outside S contribute zero; weights "computed over the original top-k set (not re-computed for the reduced set)" (L216-218) -> no renormalisation; a token may get fewer than k experts; if top-k ∩ S = ∅ "the MoE output is zero and only the residual connection passes through" (L222-224).
- Substitution (Eq. 8, L231-256): top-k is taken within S by g_i; missing experts "replaced by the highest-scoring available alternatives within the shortlist" (L253-254); mixing weights are "the routing probabilities over the constrained selection" (L249-251); every token gets exactly k experts. Main results: Router + Substitution (L296-298; Table 2 L683-684). Raw vs renormalised mixing weights follow the architecture ("OLMoE renormalizes; Qwen3 does not", L132-135).
- B: one global budget, "We do not adapt the budget per layer" (L577). Sweeps (L710-717): OLMoE B ∈ {8,16,24,32,40,48,56} ("the minimum of 8 ensures at least one full set of active experts"); Qwen3 B ∈ {16,24,32,48,64}; Mixtral B ∈ {2,3,4,5,6,7}. Table 1 picks, per cell, "the expert budget that maximizes throughput while keeping quality within one standard deviation of EAGLE (measured from T = 1 runs with 5 seeds)" (L271-273); the chosen B values are not stated. T=1 uses "conservative budget ranges", T=0 the full ranges (L696-699).

## 3. Setup
- Models (L262-268): OLMoE-1B-7B, 64 experts, k=8 (7B total / 1B active); Qwen3-30B-A3B, 128 experts, k=8 (30B / 3B active); Mixtral-8x7B, 8 experts, k=2 (47B / 13B active; L278 says "14B active").
- Drafters: EAGLE-3 for OLMoE and Qwen3; EAGLE-1 for Mixtral "where EAGLE-3 draft models are unavailable" (L285-287); adapters from the official EAGLE repo. Draft tree 63 tokens (main), sweep 3–511 (Table 2, L678-679; Fig. 4 plots 3, 7, 15, 31, 63, 127, 255).
- Hardware: NVIDIA A100 80GB; 1 GPU for OLMoE and Qwen3, 2 GPUs for Mixtral (Table 3, L691-694); FP16, PyTorch 2.1, CUDA 12.1 (L724-725). Batch size: "single-request inference" (L563); no numeric batch size stated anywhere.
- Benchmarks: GSM8K, MATH500 (accuracy), HumanEval, MBPP (pass@1), CNN/DailyMail (ROUGE-L F1); 80 samples per benchmark, max generation length 1024; T=0 and T=1 (T=1 = mean of 5 seeds) (L273-280; Table 2 L676-686).
- Baselines: AR and EAGLE(-3). All methods, AR and EAGLE included, run the authors' batched-matmul MoE kernel, which is 1.4–1.5× faster than the reference EAGLE sequential expert loop (Table 4, L741-754, L777-781). Footnote 3 (L295-301): EAGLE's implementation uses q(x)=1, so T=1 is not lossless; MoE-Spec inherits the same shift.

## 5. Limitations and failure cases (Sec. 6, L551-589, plus results text)
- B is a per-deployment hyperparameter; "very aggressive budgets can degrade quality" (L553-557). Ranking overhead 2–3%; "for very small draft trees, this overhead may outweigh the savings from reduced expert loading" (L559-562).
- Batching: "We evaluate single-request inference on A100 GPUs, but batch inference changes the tradeoffs: when multiple requests share experts, the marginal cost of loading additional experts decreases, so expert budgeting may provide smaller gains in high-throughput batch serving scenarios" (L562-567). No batched measurements.
- Routers: only top-k softmax; DeepSeek-V3-style sigmoid routing with aux-loss-free balancing "may need adaptation" of Eq. 5 (L569-576). No per-layer budgets (L577-581). Router scores experts independently; co-activation modelling left to future work (L583-587).
- Fine-grained MoEs: the fine-grained models covered are OLMoE (64, k=8) and Qwen3 (128, k=8); nothing with k>8 or N>128. Gains are smallest on them (OLMoE avg 6%, Qwen3 16% vs Mixtral 27%, L278-281), OLMoE is the most sensitive, and Qwen3 HumanEval T=0 loses 4 pts (82 -> 78) even at the "comparable quality" operating point (Table 1). Mixtral's baseline is EAGLE-1, not EAGLE-3.
- Static ranking (permanent pruning) collapses at B<=16 on math (L455-457). The T=1 "comparable to EAGLE" tolerance is measured against an EAGLE baseline that itself drops up to 11 pts vs AR (OLMoE GSM8K 60 -> 49) because of the q(x)=1 shift (L295-301). Quality values at the tightest budgets are shown only as curves, never printed.

## 6. Specific questions
- (a) Top-1/top-2 protection: NOT IN TEXT; no per-token guarantee. The shortlist is tree-level only. Truncation: "In the extreme case where Tk (ht ) ∩ S = ∅, the MoE output is zero and only the residual connection passes through" (L222-224). Substitution: "those experts are replaced by the highest-scoring available alternatives within the shortlist" (L253-254). Only floor: B>=k ("the minimum of 8 ensures at least one full set of active experts", L714-715).
- (b) Main results use substitution: "All main results use router-based expert selection with the substitution policy" (L296-298). Truncation appears only in Fig. 5b: "Both policies perform comparably across all benchmarks and expert budgets" (L455-456, right column); no truncation numbers printed. Substitution was chosen "to maintain a constant number of active experts (k) per token" (L456-457).
- (c) B per model (Table 3 L691-694; L710-717): OLMoE 8–56 of 64 (12.5–87.5% of N), Qwen3 16–64 of 128 (12.5–50% of N), Mixtral 2–7 of 8 (25–87.5% of N). Per-cell B for Table 1: not stated. Fraction of unique experts: only OLMoE has unique counts, EAGLE-3 39/layer at tree 31 and 54 at tree 255 (L336-337, L397), so B=32 is ≈82% / ≈59% of the unique set (computed here); Qwen3 and Mixtral unique counts not stated.
- (d) Qwen3-30B-A3B, single request on 1×A100 80GB FP16 ("We evaluate single-request inference on A100 GPUs", L563): Table 1 row, speedup vs AR, T=0: 2.4/2.2/2.2/1.7/1.9× (EAGLE-3 1.9/1.8/1.8/1.7/1.5×); T=1: 2.1/2.3/1.9/1.7/1.7× (EAGLE-3 1.8/1.8/1.8/1.7/1.5×), order GSM8K/MATH500/HumanEval/MBPP/CNN-DM. Absolute tok/s for Qwen3: not stated.
- (e) Tightest budgets: no Router numbers printed for B=8 (OLMoE), B=16 (Qwen3), B=2 (Mixtral); only curves (Figs. 3, 5, 9, 10). Printed statements: Static gives "near-zero accuracy on GSM8K and MATH500" at B<=16 (L455-457); Mixtral ≈78% of AR quality at maximum speedup, Qwen3 ≈95% at 2.0× (L341-345); Router is "within 5–10% of AR" only for B>=32 (L447).
- (f) Unique experts per verification step before/after: yes, OLMoE only (Fig. 4a): "at tree size of 31, MoE-Spec uses on average 29 experts compared to 39 for EAGLE-3" (L336-337); at tree 255, 54 vs 32 (L397). Not reported for Qwen3 or Mixtral.
