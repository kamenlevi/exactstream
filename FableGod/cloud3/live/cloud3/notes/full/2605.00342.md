## 2. Mechanism
1. The EAGLE-3 draft tree is built as usual (steps=4, topk=8) and pruned to the top draft_tokens=32 nodes by cumulative draft score; because a child's cumulative score cannot exceed its parent's, every top-k prefix is ancestor-closed, i.e. a valid tree (§3.2.1, l.264-280).
2. Signal: per-node Score(v) = product of drafter conditional probabilities along the root->v path (Eq. 7, l.199-202). Estimated accepted length of the top-k prefix is the prefix cumsum of sorted scores, E^[A(T_k)] = sum Score(v) (Eq. 8, l.240-244); draft probabilities stand in for the target acceptance probabilities of the exact Eq. 6 (l.180-198).
3. Cost: C(k) = latency of a whole speculative iteration when the top-k nodes are verified, profiled offline at initialization for every k in {1..draft_tokens} (32 measurements per k) and stored in a lookup table (§3.2.3, l.277-285, l.858-860). C_AR is constant for a fixed target, so it drops out of the argmax (Eq. 9, l.250-258).
4. Rule, applied at every decoding step before target verification: k* = argmax_{1<=k<=draft_tokens} E^[A(T_k)] / C(k) (Eq. 10, l.260-265); only T_{k*} is verified. There is no threshold or tunable knob for the truncation ("hyperparameter-free"); the tree shape (steps/topk/draft_tokens) is inherited from EAGLE-3.
5. Runtime work = prefix cumsum, element-wise divide by C(k), argmax (l.311-317), fused into the draft CUDA graph; one target-verify CUDA graph is pre-captured per verification length and dispatched without recapture (§3.3, l.332-339, l.304-311).
6. No training: stock EAGLE-3 draft checkpoints (Table 5, l.875-879). Lossless: the sampling verification of §2.1 (l.129-185) is untouched, only fewer nodes enter it; "remains fully lossless without modifying the target model's verification semantics" (l.566-568). The full tree k=K stays a candidate, so EVICT can keep EAGLE-3's budget when truncation is not beneficial (l.979-985).

## 3. Setup
- Targets (C.2, l.840-856): Qwen3-30B-A3B (30B total / 3B active, 128 experts, 8 active per token); Qwen3-235B-A22B (235B / 22B, 128 experts, 8 per token); Ling-flash-2.0 (103B / 6.1B, 256 experts, 8 per token); dense Hunyuan-7B-Instruct as a control.
- Drafting: EAGLE-3 draft models "from SpecBundle (Li et al., 2026)" (l.337-338; the reference entry is SpecForge). Tree config for EAGLE-3, DDD and EVICT: steps=4, topk=8, draft_tokens=32 (l.917-919); EVICT truncates this 32-node tree per step. The batched study also applies EVICT to the DFlash drafter (l.403-404, Table 6).
- Baselines (C.3, l.868-908): Vanilla AR, Lookahead (trie-based, no draft model), EAGLE-3, DDD (confidence-based dynamic depth, reimplemented on the EAGLE-3 pipeline in SGLang, l.923-928).
- Batch: all main results batch_size=1 in SGLang with CUDA graphs (l.334-336); C.5/Table 6: BS=4,8,16,32,64 in a vLLM setup with piecewise CUDA graphs, QA and HumanEval only (l.930-937; the target model used for Table 6 is not stated).
- Hardware: NVIDIA A100 SXM4 80GB; Ling-flash-2.0 at TP=4, Qwen3-235B-A22B at TP=8 (l.911-916).
- Benchmarks: MT-Bench, Alpaca, GSM8K, HumanEval, QA (Natural Questions), CNN/DM; temperature 0 and 1 (l.323-330). Metrics: mean accepted tokens (MAT) and token/s (l.339-340, l.397).

## 5. Limitations and failure cases stated by the authors
- Estimation robustness: both estimators (profiled average cost, draft-confidence benefit) are validated only on QA/HumanEval; "runtime conditions and domain shift may still affect their accuracy" (l.595-605).
- Batched: "EVICT's benefit at larger batch sizes is workload dependent" — gains persist through BS=64 on low-MAT QA, but on high-MAT HumanEval EVICT "remains broadly comparable to the untruncated backbones" (l.606-615). Table 6 actually shows EAGLE-3+EVICT below EAGLE-3 on HumanEval at BS=8/16/32/64 and DFlash+EVICT below DFlash at BS=4; the text calls this "largely preserves" (l.919-921).
- Regime: EVICT "primarily targets the expert-unsaturated, memory-bound regime" (l.167-170, Appendix A l.764-767); "reduced headroom in high-acceptance or compute-bound regimes" (l.613-615). Dense-target gains are smaller than on MoE (l.986-989).
- Systems dependence: in SGLang eager mode the extra CPU-side selection work is "entirely offset" and EVICT loses to EAGLE-3 (l.452-465); the method needs pre-captured per-length verify graphs and draft-graph fusion to pay off.
- Accepted length always falls vs EAGLE-3 (84.7%-90.1% of EAGLE-3's MAT, §4.3 l.405-420): the speedup comes from cheaper steps, not from more tokens per step.
- Single request vs batched: headline numbers are batch_size=1 in SGLang; batch results are a different stack (vLLM) and only two benchmarks. Chains vs trees: only tree drafting (EAGLE-3) and the DFlash drafter are evaluated; chain speculation appears only as prior work (Saxena et al., 2025, "coarse-grained control of conventional chain-based speculation", l.557-560). No chain-vs-tree experiment.

## 6. Specific questions
(a) Unique experts per verification step vs tree size: no table. Figure 1 plots it for Qwen3-30B-A3B over tree size 0-32 (experts axis 10-60 with label "Total Experts = 64", latency axis 8-22 ms): "Average activated experts and per-iteration latency versus tree size of Qwen3-30B-A3B ... Larger trees activate more experts, thus incur higher iteration latency." (l.45-51). Figure 5(b) plots active experts vs MAT with the 32.5% reduction (l.451-454). The only closed form is Eq. 11, borrowed from MoESD under independent, roughly uniform routing: "Nuniq(t) = M[1 - (1 - K/M)^t]" (l.783-791). Not reconciled in the text: the figures say 64 total experts while C.2 says Qwen3-30B-A3B has 128.
(b) Relation to truncating a chain by running draft confidence: the objective is the chain-based utility rule of Saxena et al. lifted to a tree — "Following prior work (Saxena et al., 2025), we define the expected speculation utility as U(k) := C_AR E^[A(T_k)] / C(k)" (l.247-253). Score(v) is the product of draft probabilities along the root->v path (l.199-202), so on a single chain it is exactly the running draft confidence and the prefix cumsum is the chain's estimated accepted length. A cut-off on confidence alone is the ablation that loses: "both the score-coverage variants and DDD are cost-agnostic strategies that rely only on drafter-side confidence, and they consistently underperform EVICT" (l.525-531); EAGLE-3 is the special case rho = 1 (l.519-523).
(c) Fraction of verified tokens not committed in the baselines: not stated as a fraction. Printed inputs: EAGLE-3 verifies a fixed "draft_tokens=32" tree every step (l.917-919) with average MAT 3.65 / 3.52 on Qwen3-30B-A3B (Table 2), i.e. about 11% of verified nodes are committed and about 89% are not (3.65/32 = 11.4%); Ling-flash-2.0 (3.94/32) and Qwen3-235B-A22B (3.57/32) are similar. For EVICT: "EVICT verifies 74.7% fewer tokens than EAGLE-3 across the entire MAT range on average" (l.427-428), i.e. roughly 8 verified nodes per step for MAT 3.09, so roughly 60% uncommitted. Caveat: whether the 32 and the MAT count the root/bonus token is not stated.
