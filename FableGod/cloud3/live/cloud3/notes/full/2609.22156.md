## 2. The bound, precisely
- Verification step = chain of K draft tokens + 1 bonus position, m = K+1, B = 1 (l.228-231). The only routing signal is
  the layer-averaged union of unique experts Ūr = (1/|L_MoE|) Σ_ℓ |U_ℓ,r| (Eq. 5, l.197), U_ℓ,r = union of expert ids over
  all m positions of the pass in layer ℓ (Eq. A2, l.744). Intuition TT,r ≈ αŪr + β (Eq. 3, l.222); fitted model
  TT,r = β0(mr) + α(mr)Ūr + cL_ctx,r + εr (Eq. 4, l.242), β0 and α free per m, c shared. TD(K) = (0.459K + 0.073) ms (Eq. 6, l.243).
- Expert-loading growth vs draft length is NOT modelled (no independent-routing or union-growth formula): Ūr is measured
  per round from router traces in a vLLM fork (l.204-208), "treated as an observed cost signal, not as a causally
  identified volume of traffic" (l.211-213). The empirical growth is Table A1 (§4). Tree drafting: not used.
- Coefficients: α(m) = 0.078–0.084 ms/expert for m ≤ 4, 0.264–0.268 for m ≥ 5, ratio ≈ 3.2 (l.798-800); β0 7.3→10.5 ms
  then 4.7 ms (l.801-802); c ≈ 1.1 ms per 1000 context tokens (l.810). Bandwidth check: "48 layers of 9.4 MB each, or
  about 0.45 GB at 2 TB/s, gives about 0.23 ms", matching α at m ≥ 5 (l.778-781).
- Speedup (after MoESD): SpeedUp ≈ E[Gr] / ( K·TD/TT(0) + 1/η(K) ), η_B(K) = TT^(B)(0)/TT^(B)(K) (Eq. 1-2, l.150-182).
  SD loses when "the gain in E[Gr]" fails to offset "the growth of the denominator" (l.185-186); 1/η(K) grows with Ūr (l.170-176).
- The bound itself is numerical: an offline Oracle solves a Stochastic Shortest Path over the reference text,
  dp[i] = min_k Σ_a P(a|k)[Cost(i,k,a) + dp[min(i+a+1,N)]] (Eq. 8, l.332-339), P(a|k) = Π_{j<a} p_j (1−p_a) from draft
  confidence (Eq. 7, l.326), Cost(i,k,a) = t_draft(k) + t_target(U, L_prefix), U from counterfactual "junk tails"
  (l.303-307). S = Cost_AR/dp[0] (l.356) is "an efficiency limit only for reproducing this specific text" (l.357, 401),
  "(an upper bound)" for online heuristics (l.404).
- Local optimality: rejected alternatives in Delta Space lie beyond the line Δ[Cost] = 1.85 · Δ[a] (Fig. 3, l.457; from
  the 1st percentile of ΔCost/Δa, l.488); ΔE[Cost]/ΔE[a] "must not exceed a constant threshold" (l.481-483), cost
  there counted in "number of activated experts" (l.414-416).
## 3. Setup
- Target: Qwen/Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct, "MoE with 128 experts and top-ke = 8 routing" (l.646-648). The only model.
- Draft: lmsys/SGLang-EAGLE3-Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct-SpecForge (l.649-651); linear chain of K draft tokens (l.115);
  "The draft head is dense, so no routing term enters the model." (l.899). No tree drafting.
- Hardware/stack: one NVIDIA A100 80GB, bfloat16, vLLM fork with per-round CUDA-event trace, FULL CUDA graphs, B = 1
  synchronous, T = 0.7, top_p = 1.0, seed 0, max_model_len 4096, gpu_mem_util 0.85, max_new_tokens 1000 (l.652-658).
- Calibration: SpecBench, 560 questions, 6 domains; K ∈ {1..10}, one run per K, "about nine GPU-hours"; 925 752 rounds,
  641 generations per run (l.659-665). Oracle study: SpecBench Math Reasoning, 80 questions (l.408-410). App. B trace:
  39 responses, 9 554 decisions, Lmax = 8 (l.1011-1012). Batch sizes: B = 1 only (l.163, 231, 654).

## 5. Mitigations (EVICT, MoE-Spec, AcceptMoE, others)
- l.113-117: "EVICT and MoE-Spec directly exploit the growth of the activated-expert union: EVICT prunes the
  candidate tree, and MoE-Spec caps the per-layer expert budget (Pan et al., 2026; McDanel et al., 2026)."
- l.117-121: "EcoSpec incorporates the predicted marginal cost of expert activation into draft-token selection
  (Xie et al., 2026), while Sparse Verification sparsifies the computation of verification itself"
- l.147-150: "The paper does not propose an online policy and does not claim superiority over EVICT, MoE-Spec,
  EcoSpec, or other adaptive methods." l.126-127: the unique-experts/cost link "is therefore not claimed here as novel."
- l.109-113: MoESD "introduces target-model efficiency, and Cascade weighs expected progress against verification cost".
  l.130-137: SpecMoE, SP-MoE, SpecMoEOff, MoE-SpeQ "change the expert-loading regime". l.103-105: DISCO, GammaTune
  "do not directly model the MoE-specific signal of expert-union size". AcceptMoE: not mentioned anywhere.
## 6. Stated limitations
- Batch: "Generation at B > 1 introduces inter-sequence expert sharing, which fundamentally changes the structure of
  the overhead and requires a different cost model." (l.503-506). "Cross-hardware, cross-system, and batched
  transferability were not tested; the model requires recalibration when the environment changes or at B > 1." (l.1032-1037)
- Domain: Math Reasoning only; "the exact budget saturation point (Lmax) will vary for text with different entropy."
  (l.509-520). Oracle approximations (l.522-531); no online policy (l.533-539); single seed, one run per K, m = 4/5
  cause untested, tracing overhead unmeasured (l.1026-1031); regression "does not establish causality" (l.813-814).
- Consecutive tokens of one sequence sharing experts: measured only implicitly — Ūr is the per-pass union (Table A1:
  12.1 → 30.2 for m = 2 → 11, far below 8·m) and App. B counts "new experts per layer" a step brings in (l.1025-1028;
  panel values 0.38–5.94). No overlap rate, sharing model, or growth exponent is stated.
- Fine-grained MoE: single 128-expert/top-8 target (l.647); the term and any cross-model comparison: not stated. Memory-
  bound single-user: motivated at l.43-46, l.194-197 ("memory traffic tied to expert weights"); all runs B = 1; the
  words "memory-bound"/"single-user" never appear.
## 7. Specific questions
- (a) Overlap / union-growth exponent across draft tokens: not stated. Only medians: "Median Ūr" 12.1 (K=1) … 30.2
  (K=10) (Table A1, l.712-722) and "new experts increases from 2.66 to 3.12 experts per layer" (l.1452-1454).
- (b) Draft length at which verification cost doubles (Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B): not stated. My arithmetic on Table A1
  medians: 13.67/8.98 = 1.52× from K=1 to K=10, so no doubling inside the sweep; TT(0) is never printed.
- (c) Same cost per unique expert regardless of token count: yes, within a regime. H2: "one additional unique expert
  is associated with an approximately constant increment in latency." (l.685-687); linear in Ūr (Eq. 4, l.242); Emax adds
  only "+0.015 ± 0.002" ms (l.767). Caveat: α depends on microbatch m (≈3.2× jump at m = 4→5, l.798-800), not on tokens/expert.
