## 2. Mechanism (precisely)
- Inference-only, inside vLLM v0.10.1 (v1 scheduler): intercepts the router output at every MoE layer, 4 fused Triton kernels in the critical path, CUDA-graph compatible (l.65-67, 709-714). "requires no calibration data, makes no permanent changes to the model" (l.65-66); no training, fine-tuning or offline profiling (l.705-706).
- Phase-aware optimizer (in the batch scheduler) applies LYNX only to decode-only, memory-bound batches; prefill and batches containing a prefill chunk are bypassed entirely (l.423-445, 693-694).
- Confidence analyzer ("AffinityBinning"): per token, affinity of each expert = log-ratio of its softmax probability to the top-1 expert's (= logit difference; pre-sigmoid score difference for sigmoid routers); discretised into bins, 0 = top-1, increasingly negative as the gap widens (l.527-539).
- Two hyperparameters alpha (bin width inversely proportional) and beta (bounds the bin count), "set once per model architecture based on its sparsity ratio (k/N)" (l.540-544). Numeric values: not stated.
- Adaptive expert scorer: each expert's router logit gets a score from how often other tokens in the batch preferred it; weight = (batch size) ^ (bin value), i.e. exponential in the bin with base = batch size (l.585-603). The number of active experts is chosen dynamically from the score distribution, bin width and allowed bin count (l.604-608). High-confidence tokens always keep their top-ranked expert (l.608-629); shared experts always kept with score 1 (l.635-639).
- Expert remapper: low-confidence token-to-expert assignments are redirected to experts in the surviving set; top-k semantics preserved (l.643-664). Kernel steps: token-wise binning; batch-wise scoring; expert pruning (per batch); recompute softmax and top-k; compact active-expert list, assign tokens, renormalise weights (l.724-734).
- Per-layer: sensitivity varies by layer (Fig. 9) and is said to "enable layer-adaptive remapping policies" (l.683); whether the shipped policy actually differs per layer: not stated.

## 5. Limitations and failure cases stated
- Chunked prefill with a memory-bound prefill chunk is "a known edge case; addressing them risks degrading time-to-first-token (TTFT) and is left to future work" (l.439-442). Prefill is never remapped; remapping there collapses accuracy (Fig. 4).
- Mixtral gains smallest, "(1.09-1.18x) due to fewer total experts which limits remapping search space" (l.800-802). EP=4 gains shrink to 1.09x because fewer experts per GPU leave "fewer placement configurations for LYNX to exploit" (l.1001-1004).
- Worst accuracy cell -1.0% (Qwen3-30B HE). Online dynamic pruning + extreme quantization works excluded because their "accuracy drops vary drastically on hard tasks such as GSM8K and HumanEval" (l.1087-1092). Kernel dispatch parameters not yet tuned for variable expert counts (l.1119-1121). The §3 figures do not name which model each panel uses.
- Fine-grained MoEs: evaluated N=64/k=8 (Qwen2-57B), 128-expert Qwen3-30B and Llama-4-Maverick; DeepSeek-V3 (k=8, N=256) named only as a sparsity example (l.118), not evaluated. Single-request decode: not evaluated with LYNX (Fig. 1b: at batch 1 the baseline MoE already matches the small dense model, l.182-185). Speculative decoding: "LYNX can work on top of speculative decoding, to lower overhead of verification" (l.1130-1133), asserted in Related Work, not measured.

## 6. Specific questions
- (a) SUBSTITUTE, not drop: experts are pruned from the batch's active set, tokens are remapped. "LYNX remaps low-affinity token-to-expert assignments within a batch onto experts the batch is already activating" (l.87-89); "every token still activates exactly k experts, and no expert is permanently discarded" (l.662-664).
- (b) Yes. "the final kernel compacts the active-expert list, assigns tokens, and renormalizes weights" (l.733-734), after "recomputing softmax and top-k" (l.732-733).
- (c) Prefill vs decode, Fig. 4 (l.358-397; rows prefill Keep/Drop, cols decode Keep/Drop): HumanEval 37.8% (Keep/Keep), 30.5% (Keep prefill, Drop decode), 12.2% (Drop prefill, Keep decode), 6.1% (Drop/Drop); GSM8K 58.5% / 53.1% / 11.5% / 9.2%. Caption: "sensitive to expert reassignment during prefill but resilient during decode" (l.394-395). Per layer, Fig. 9 (layers 0-30 on the axis, reference "No Mask (43.6%)", accuracy axis 40-55%): "certain layers tolerate aggressive reduction while others require expert fidelity" (l.681-683); no per-layer values printed. Rank, Fig. 7 GSM8K: denying Rank-0 gives 0.58 / 0.26 / 0.03 across the three models vs 0.81 / 0.64 / 0.47 for Rank-1 and >= 0.63 for Rank-2..5 (series labels not recoverable from layout); Fig. 8 GSM8K cumulative keep: E0 only 0.60 / 0.34 -> E0-1 0.72 / 0.55 -> E0-2 0.73 / 0.59 -> E0-3 0.77 / 0.67 -> E0-4 0.79 / 0.68 (Qwen2-57B and DeepSeek-V2 series); "Most performance is recovered after keeping 3-4 experts" (l.641). Fig. 6: accuracy gap between remapping confident vs uncertain tokens is 8-9 points (Mixtral, baseline 58.5%) and 13-27 points (Qwen, baseline 82%) at 20-50% active tokens.
- (d) No ~1.5x operating point exists in this version; the maximum p50 speedup is 1.30x. Fastest cells: GSM8K Qwen2-57B 1.30x at 78.0->77.2% (-0.8%); HumanEval Qwen2-57B 1.25x at 65.9->65.9% (+0.0%), DeepSeek-Coder-V2 1.22x at 76.8->78.7% (+1.9%) (Table 2, l.760, 770).
- (e) "maximum batch size set to 16 during decode" (l.777-778); uncapped for timestamped traces (l.778-780); sweeps 8/16/32/64 (Fig. 17), 4/8/16 (Fig. 12a), 8/16/32 (Fig. 18).
- (f) Not stated numerically for LYNX. Closest: "reducing CPU-side activated experts by 25%" (Fiddler, l.1019-1020), "Even at 75% activated experts" (offloading, l.1013), the Fig. 5 illustration "two out of four activated in this example" (l.498). ## 7. Verification of secondary-source claims (against v3)
- "up to 1.55x speedup on GSM8K and HumanEval": WRONG. Text: "up to 1.30x lower latency" (l.31, 72, 709); GSM8K max 1.30x, HumanEval max 1.25x p50 (Table 2), 1.29x median disaggregated (Fig. 10). "1.55" occurs only inside "11.55" ms (l.768).
- "vs NAEE on GSM8K, Lynx > 55% accuracy at 1.5x where NAEE < 40%": NOT IN TEXT. "NAEE" never appears; ref [42] (Lu et al., "Not All Experts are Equal") is cited only as a per-token/voting prior (l.347-348, 672-674). No 1.5x point and no such comparison exist in v3.
- "router confidence indicates which token-expert mappings matter": CONFIRMED, Insight 2 "Router confidence provides a reliable signal for identifying critical token-expert mappings" (l.558-561). "primary expert choices matter much more than secondary": CONFIRMED, Insight 3 "The top-ranked expert dominates output quality; lower-ranked experts among the top-k exhibit high redundancy" (l.684-686; Figs. 7-8). "prefill highly sensitive, decode resilient": CONFIRMED, Insight 1 (l.416-420) and Fig. 4 (l.393-397).
