## 2. Mechanism
- Similarity = activation-based, offline. For each calibration batch and each MoE layer l, run ALL M experts on the layer's input X^(l-1), A_j = E_j(X^(l-1)); accumulate pairwise Sim(A_p, A_q) over N batches, divide by N -> one M x M matrix S^(l) per layer, normalised to [0,1] (Eq. 1, Alg. 1, lines 306-328, 1356-1391).
- Metric used: Frobenius similarity M_fro(E,F) = 1 - ||X_E - X_F||_F / max over all pairs (Eqs. 10-11, lines 1166-1177); so by construction the least similar pair is ~0 and the most similar ~1. Cosine and CKA (linear/RBF/poly) tried (Table 4); weight-based "Concat"/"Logic" (theta3(theta1*theta2)) tried and worse (Table 5).
- Re-routing, per layer, per forward step (Sec. 3.2.2, lines 415-460): Step 1 primary set E_p = union over all tokens in the batch of each token's top-S router experts, S in [1,K) (Eq. 2; S=1 or 2 in experiments, written "Top-1"/"Top-2"). Step 2: every secondary expert E_u (in some token's top-K but not in E_p): sim*_u = max_{v in E_p} S_uv; if sim*_u >= rho, ALL tokens that chose E_u are sent to E_{v*} = argmax (Eqs. 3-4). Step 3: active set = E_p plus secondary experts with sim*_u < rho (Eq. 5).
- Routing weight: "the re-routing process does not modify the router weights" (line 448); the kernel rewrites only the expert-index tensor I (T x K), W is untouched (Alg. 2, lines 1423-1464). The token's weight for the dropped expert is therefore applied to the substitute's output; not renormalised, not dropped. If the substitute is already one of the token's own experts, that expert is simply evaluated twice under two weights (not discussed in the text).
- Critical-expert protection: a secondary expert whose max similarity to any primary expert is < rho is kept active ("critical"); kernel keeps the original only "if rho > 0 and sbest < rho" (line 1456). rho=0 disables protection.
- Batch-level trigger: none; SERE runs on every MoE layer at every step, in prefill and decode (a decode-only variant is worse, Table 13). Batch dependence comes only from E_p being the union over the batch; "skipping more experts when redundancy is high and fewer when diversity is essential" (lines 193-194).
- Hyperparameters: S (1 or 2), rho (0.3 for Qwen1.5 and DeepSeekV2-Lite, 0.5 for Qwen3 in the main tables; swept 0.0-1.0), similarity metric, calibration set.
- Calibration: FineWeb-Edu, 400 sequences x 128 tokens, no retraining; Frobenius calibration takes 28 s on Qwen1.5 (Table 4, line 780). Re-routing overhead 6 us per layer at batch 16-64 vs MLP 137-233 us (Fig. 7c, lines 681-698).

## 3. Setup
- Models (Table 8, lines 1491-1507): Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat: 60 experts/layer, 4 routed + 4 shared, 14.3B/2.7B active, 24 MoE layers. DeepSeekV2-Lite: 64 experts, 6 routed + 2 shared, 16B/2.4B, 26 of 27 layers MoE. Qwen3-30B-A3B: 128 experts, top-8, 30B/3B, 48 layers.
- Baselines: HC-SMoE (merging, pruning rate matched to TPOT), direct Top-K reduction (Yang et al. 2025b), LYNX (batch-aware skipping).
- Speed: vLLM, one NVIDIA H20 GPU per model, QPS 8/16/24/32, input/output fixed 128/32 tokens, 5,000 requests per test, metric TPOT (ms); TTFT in Appendix C.2.
- Accuracy: OpenCompass, batch size 16, temperature 0.7/0.3/0.7, max output 1024/1024/2048. Benchmarks: CMMLU, BoolQ (both CoT), BBH; MATH, GSM8K, Math401; HumanEval, MBPP.
- Note: the "K" in result tables is SERE's S (primary experts kept per token), not the model's top-k.

## 5. Limitations and failure cases stated by the authors
- No "Limitations" section. Stated: no FLOP reduction, so "not expected to provide noticeable speedups in the compute-bound prefill stage" (lines 1656-1657); decode-only application causes a "distribution shift" and underperforms (lines 1728-1731).
- Threshold sensitivity: "DeepSeekV2 maintains relatively stable performance across different settings, whereas Qwen3 and Qwen1.5 exhibit notable performance fluctuations" (lines 718-719); math/code "require higher similarity threshold" than exam tasks (lines 1625-1630).
- S=1 is lossy on Qwen1.5 (39.82 vs 48.52) and Qwen3 (64.11 vs 82.24); the "almost no performance loss" wording is tied to S=2.
- Fine-grained MoEs: Qwen3 (128 experts, top-8) is the only such model evaluated; the authors attribute baselines' better showing there to "more fine-grained and redundant experts", vs Qwen1.5/DeepSeekV2 with "fewer, more specialized experts" that "are thus more sensitive to expert selection" (lines 576-578). No model with top-k > 8 or > 128 experts is measured; DeepSeekMoE, Ling-mini-2.0, OLMoE appear only as similarity heatmaps (Figs. 9-14, lines 1737-1740), no accuracy or speed.
- Dissimilar-expert models: not evaluated; the only guard is rho (critical experts retained, fewer skipped). Shared experts (Qwen1.5, DeepSeek) are untouched by the method text.
- Single-request decode: not stated / not measured; lowest load is QPS=8. By Eqs. 2-5 a one-token batch keeps its top-S experts plus any secondary expert with sim* < rho; no numbers given.

## 6. Specific questions
(a) Similarity distribution. Only Qwen3-30B-A3B, qualitative: "Layer-1 has the highest average similarity, with nearly all pairs above 0.9, while Layer-6 has the lowest average similarity, with most pairs below 0.4" (lines 364-365); "Expert 92 stands out as a critical expert, with a similarity of less than 0.1 to all others" (lines 367-368). Per-layer averages are plotted in Fig. 4 but not printed. Mean cosine / share of pairs above a threshold: not stated for any model; Qwen1.5, DeepSeekV2-Lite and others: heatmaps only (Figs. 9-14), not stated. Metric behind Fig. 4 not named (Frobenius is the default, line 498).
(b) Accuracy vs re-routing ratio. There is no explicit ratio knob; the curve is over rho (and S). "Up to a point, increasing the threshold improves accuracy while adding only negligible decoding latency. Beyond that point, accuracy continues to rise, but the speedup drops sharply" (lines 714-715). Table 10 (Qwen3, S=1, avg acc / TPOT ms; lines 1632-1643): rho 0.0 52.27/28.04; 0.1 52.83/29.19; 0.2 53.27/30.72; 0.3 54.68/29.21; 0.4 57.16/29.81; 0.5 64.11/33.10; 0.6 72.53/34.28; 0.7 80.95/35.37; 0.8 81.86/38.92; 0.9 81.76/46.02; 1.0 82.56/44.54. Fig. 8 curves for all three models carry no printed numbers. S=1 vs S=2 (rho>0, avg): Qwen1.5 39.82 vs 47.25, DeepSeek 55.62 vs 55.48, Qwen3 64.11 vs 80.37.
(c) Dependence on similarity. Yes, by premise and by ablation: "many experts within an MoE layer exhibit high functional similarity. Therefore, SERE re-routes tokens from a subset of experts to their most similar counterparts" (lines 185-186); re-routing to the least-similar expert gives avg 4.72 (S=1) / 28.74 (S=2) vs 37.87 / 47.15 (Table 7); Theorem 1 bounds the output error by the expert distance (lines 1282-1297). On models where experts are not similar the authors say only that the design skips "fewer when diversity is essential for accuracy" (line 194) and assert "high expert similarity is common in MoE models, regardless of whether upcycling initialization (Komatsuzaki et al., 2023) is employed" (lines 372-373); no dissimilar-expert model is tested.

## 7. Verification of secondary-source claims
- "up to 2.0x speedup against the PyTorch version at QPS 24 on Qwen1.5-MoE": WRONG. The 2.0x is SERE-CUDA (S=1) vs the unmodified model: 39.3 -> 19.7 ms TPOT at QPS=24 (Fig. 6; "the acceleration ratio reaches up to 2.0x when QPS= 24", line 592). Against SERE's own PyTorch version at that point the figure reads 22.1 -> 19.7 ms (~1.1x); the paper's only CUDA-vs-PyTorch figure is "approximately 1.5x" with no QPS attached (line 655).
- "minimal quality loss on complex reasoning benchmarks": CONFIRMED as the authors' wording (abstract lines 36-39: "complex reasoning benchmarks ... up to 2.0x speedup with minimal quality loss"). Holds for S=2 with rho>0 (avg -1.27 / +0.72 / -1.87 points); not for S=1 on Qwen1.5 (-8.70) or Qwen3 (-18.13), and Qwen3 mbpp loses 6.40 points even at S=2.
- "a single-line vLLM integration": CONFIRMED as stated: "plug-and-play use in vLLM with only a single-line code change" (line 36; also lines 196-198, 477-478). The paper shows no code; the kernel rewrites the top-k index tensor between router and expert execution (Alg. 2).
