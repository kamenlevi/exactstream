## 2. The two metrics
- Notation: A(e,T) = 0/1 activation sequence of expert e on input T; f(e,T,p,m) = Σ_{i=p}^{p+m-1} A(e,T)[i], the number of tokens in the m-token window at p that activate e (line 1791).
- SRP (Segment Routing best Performance), single expert: a segment router R_e^m must predict e as all-active or all-inactive over each window (Eq. 2, line 1780); every window of every input is a binary task with m samples. F1(R) = 2·ΣR·f / Σ[m·R + f] (Eq. 3, lines 1785-1789). SRP(e,m) = max_R F1(R) (line 1794); the maximiser predicts active iff f ≥ α_e^m, giving SRP(e,m) = 2·Σ_{f≥α} f / Σ[m·I[f≥α] + f] (Eq. 4, lines 1798-1801). "Intrinsic property of the expert", independent of any routing method (line 1802).
- SRP, expert group E (layer or model): same construction with one segment router deciding every e ∈ E per window (Eqs. 5-6, lines 1810-1827). The segment router's number of active experts is not capped (line 293); supplementary "segment routing size ratio" ρ̂ = avg #experts activated by the segment router / avg #activated by the original router (lines 295-297). Tables print SRP ×100 (54.14); prose uses fractions ("> 0.5").
- SCH (Segment Cache best Hit rate): an oracle cache with cache ratio ρ = cache size / number of activated experts (line 320); per token it loads the demanded experts not in cache and evicts the same number of unused experts, choosing the ones "activated the least times in the next m tokens" (lines 321-323); SCH = its hit rate (line 324). Decoding stage only ("We only describe the decoding stage", footnote †, line 380). The true upper bound is the clairvoyant/Belady algorithm (lines 326-332, 3215).
- Segment lengths: m = 4, 16, 64, 256 (Tables 6/7/8, lines 2128, 2737, 2753); headline numbers at m = 16. Cache sizes: SCH simulated "on every possible cache size" (line 1163); reported at ρ = 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 (Table 7, line 2737), Figure 8 sweeps ρ from 0 to 4 (line 1193), Table 8 is at ρ = 2, Table 4 at ρ = 1.0/2.0/3.0.

## 4. Main results
- Segment length: most models have similar SRP and ρ̂ at m = 4; differences grow with m and "the relative order remains after m = 16" (lines 399-403). Four groups at m = 16 (lines 573-582): Group 1 LLaMA-MoE-v2…OLMoE, SRP > 0.5, ρ̂ ∼ 1.25; Group 2 Mixtral-8x7B…LLaMA-MoE-v1, SRP ∼ 0.48, long-term ρ̂ ∼ 2.5; Group 3 XVERSE-MoE…DeepSeekMoE, SRP ∼ 0.36, ρ̂ ∼ 2.0; Group 4 NLLB-MoE…SwitchTransformers, SRP < 0.31. Position-wise SRP is constant except at p = 0 (lines 2154-2158).
- Load balance trade-off: high-SRP REAL models are often imbalanced (LLaMA-MoE-v2 LB 29.04); TOY NoLB SRP 56.42 / LB 13.21 vs Baseline 43.56 / 4.02 vs OverLB 36.42 / 1.79 (lines 633-642). Qwen3 (54.14, LB 3.19) and GRIN-MoE (50.39, 3.89) have high SRP with moderate LB → "good global load balance" via topic-dependent expert sets (lines 645-650).
- Shared experts: "Shared experts harms local routing consistency" (line 165); "all models in groups 1 and 2 do not use shared models" (lines 654-655); TOY 1ShrExp 41.38 and 2ShrExp 38.79 vs Baseline 43.56 (lines 637-640) at similar perplexity. Reasons offered: a "bypass effect" and a "decreased size of expert combination space" (lines 657-662). Combination space: FewerExp 41.62, ActFewer 27.13 below Baseline, ActMore 55.69 above (lines 634-642), but "a less significant factor compared to load balance and shared experts" (lines 674-676). Dense/interleaved layers: DenseFst 44.87, DenseHlf 43.67 — "the lead is minor, and both models suffer from high perplexity" (lines 680-681); REAL models with dense layers all sit in groups 3-4 but "the low SRP of these models may not be due to dense layers" (lines 682-684).
- Domain-wise SRP (m = 16, relative to full corpus, lines 690-703): (1) Phi-3.5-MoE, GRIN-MoE, OLMoE: > 10% higher on ArXiv/StackExchange/GitHub, higher on OpenMath/OpenCode; (2) LLaMA-MoE-v2, Yuan2.0, Qwen3: higher on Wikipedia/generic text, lower on OpenMath/OpenCode; (3) Mixtral-8x7B, MiniCPM-MoE, JetMoE: flat, "mediocre to low SRP". All TOY models show pattern (1) → attributed to pretraining data.
- Specialisation (§4.2): domain spec = CV of an expert's activation frequency across domains; vocabulary spec per Muennighoff et al. (2025) on input / predicted / ground-truth tokens (lines 953-967, 1144-1146). Many REAL models show positive correlation between expert domain specialisation and SRP; Qwen3, Phi-3.5-MoE, GRIN-MoE, OLMoE have high SRP, high domain spec and strong correlation at once (lines 971-1052); Qwen2 and LLaMA-MoE "hardly have any domain specialization" (lines 1009-1036). Input-vocab spec: "negative or insignificant correlation" with SRP; predicted/ground-truth vocab spec "slightly positively correlated" (lines 1146-1149). Verdict: "domain specialization plays a more important role in local routing consistency than vocabulary specialization, especially on models with both high local routing consistency and global load balance" (lines 1151-1153); per-expert plots agree (lines 2882-2885).
- SCH vs cache ratio (§5.1): group 1 has the fastest-growing SCH at small ρ "as well as turning points near ρ = 2, after which they share similar SCH with group 2"; groups 3-4 grow "nearly linearly" (lines 1165-1169). "Since only group 1 models ... have turning points on SCH, we claim that in general, ρ = 2 can balance cache effectiveness and efficiency" (lines 1169-1171); conclusion: "a cache size around 2x the number of active experts" (line 1244). SRP–SCH correlation is highest at ρ = 1.5 (97.46 / 97.37 / 98.03 / 97.89 for m = 4/16/64/256) and at ρ = 2.0 is 91.91 / 94.87 / 96.15 / 96.24 (Table 7, lines 2737-2741); most models have ρ̂ ∈ [1, 3] at m ≥ 16 (line 2475).
- SCH vs real caches (Table 3, lines 1226-1231; correlation across REAL models, columns LRU / LFU / "Fixed", the last never explained in prose): m=4 81.20 / 77.39 / 76.26; m=16 90.43 / 88.70 / 89.79; m=64 93.10 / 92.82 / 95.50; m=256 97.52 / 99.20 / 97.91. Table 4 (TOY Baseline, m = 16, optimal hit rate = 100): ρ=1.0 LRU 56.49, LFU 61.92, SCH 80.97; ρ=2.0 67.04 / 70.87 / 90.55; ρ=3.0 75.26 / 78.35 / 96.23 (lines 1227-1231).
- Per-layer (App. E.6): "Most models have peak SRPs among middle layers, while some (e.g., Yuan2.0 and MiniCPM) have another peak at the last layer" (lines 2787-2788); "PowerMoE and Qwen2 have another peak on layer 2 due to expert imbalance" (line 2792); layer-wise SCH shows "the same" patterns (lines 2837-2838). Per-expert (E.7): LLaMA-MoE-v2, Yuan2.0, PowerMoE owe model-level SRP to a few very-high-frequency experts (lines 2878-2881).
- Throughput (App. F): naive LRU + on-demand loading on TOY models; relative overhead vs full-GPU correlates with SCH "positive during prefilling (r ≈ 0.2), and negative during decoding (r ≈ −0.3)" (lines 3225-3228).

## 5. Limitations stated; speculative decoding
- No section titled "Limitations". Self-stated caveats: SCH models the decoding stage only (line 380); SCH "relies on oracle information" (line 1209); throughput also depends on "cache management, overlap exploitation, hardware coordinate" (lines 3191-3192) and the TOY correlations are "not far from 0 ... the model ... should not be the sole decider" (lines 3244-3246); the combination-space rule is not followed by "some REAL models (e.g., Phi and GRIN)" (lines 675-676); the dense-layer effect is confounded (lines 682-684); the 2x insight is offered "from the aspect of model design" for devices with known memory limits (lines 232-234); models studied are 3B-57B base checkpoints.
- Speculative decoding / verifying several consecutive tokens in one pass: not stated (no occurrence of "speculative" or "draft"). Closest text: prefilling processes "multiple consecutive tokens in one run" so its bottleneck is local load balance, not consistency (lines 3229-3233); prior locality evidence covered only "2 consecutive tokens" (Jiang et al., lines 1730-1731) or "2-4 consecutive tokens" (Eliseev & Mazur, lines 1752-1753).

## 6. Specific questions
- (a) 16-token segment, Qwen3: only Qwen3-30B-A3B (128 experts, top-8, no shared) is studied. The paper never states a "fraction of the active-expert budget"; its number is the best fixed-group F1: SRP(m=16) = 54.14 — "Qwen3        54.14        all    1:16     0" (line 609). Its segment router's size ratio is given only at group level: "Group 1 (LLaMA-MoE-v2–OLMoE) has the highest SRP (> 0.5 when m = 16) and ρ̂ (∼ 1.25)" (lines 575-576). SCH(ρ=2, m=16) 95% CI = (76.49, 77.46) (line 2757).
- (b) Early vs late: neither — middle. "Most models have peak SRPs among middle layers, while some (e.g., Yuan2.0 and MiniCPM) have another peak at the last layer." (lines 2787-2788). Early-layer peaks exist only as imbalance artefacts: "PowerMoE and Qwen2 have another peak on layer 2 due to expert imbalance." (line 2792).
- (c) Shared expert + fine-grained (≥ 128 experts, top-8): NOT IN TEXT — no studied model has both (Table 5, lines 2027-2050; the 128-expert models are Qwen3-30B-A3B 8/0 shared, NLLB 2/0, SwitchTF 1/0). What is said about shared experts generally: "Shared experts harms local routing consistency, as well as expert configurations that limit expert combination space." (lines 165-166); "the existence of shared experts decreases the number of both available and activated experts, resulting in fewer possible expert combinations" (lines 660-662). Nearest data points: Qwen2-57B-A14B (64/8/8) SRP 36.74, Qwen1.5-MoE (60/4/4) 30.71, DeepSeek-V2-Lite (64/6/2) 37.92.

## 7. Verification of secondary-source claims
- "Models that place MoE on every layer and omit shared experts show the highest local routing consistency; shared experts reduce it" — CONFIRMED as an observation (Table 1: all 11 group-1/2 models are "all" + S:A 0, lines 606-618; lines 654-655; TOY 1ShrExp/2ShrExp below Baseline, lines 637-640), with a caveat: the paper attributes the low SRP of dense-interleaved models to shared experts or sparse activation, "not ... due to dense layers" (lines 682-684), and rates interleaving as having "less significant impact" (lines 678-680).
- "Most models balance cache effectiveness and efficiency with a cache around twice the number of active experts" — CONFIRMED (lines 44-46, 180-181, 191-193, 1170-1171, 1244); nuance: the ρ = 2 turning point is seen only in group 1, groups 3-4 "growing nearly linearly" (lines 1166-1170).
- "Domain-specialised experts contribute more to consistency than vocabulary-specialised ones" — CONFIRMED (lines 43-44, 1151-1153, 1242-1243, 2883-2885).
