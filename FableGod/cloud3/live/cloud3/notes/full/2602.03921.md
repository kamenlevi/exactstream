## 2. Framework and the Least-Stale (LS) policy
- Four policy dimensions: routing, prefetching, eviction, miss handling (lines 51-53). PyTorch forward hooks on each MoE gate (lines 326-328, 763-769); Algorithm 1 = Phase 1 routing modification, Phase 2 miss handling per selected expert, Phase 3 prefetch submission (lines 280-301). Main + Prefetch loops synchronous, Watchdog loop asynchronous, "constantly monitoring the prefetch queue" and issuing non-blocking CUDA copies when there is space (lines 192-194, 311-312, 787-791). Experts live in CPU pinned memory, cache = GPU (lines 781-782); bandwidth throttling and capacity limits are software-emulated (lines 798-803).
- Policies: routing = Standard or cache-aware z' = z + λ·Δavg·1_C (Eq. 1, line 234; dual-logit: modified logits pick experts, original logits weight them, lines 771-774). Miss = Fetch / Fetch-lowest-bit (int8:4) / Fetch-priority (int8→int4→int2 fallback) / Drop low-rank experts (rank 3+ per Skliar) / Substitution (lines 247-259). Eviction = temporal LRU, LFU; spatial SB (score-based), FLD (farthest layer distance); plus LHU (lines 268-271, 808-840). Prefetch = top-k × overfetch factor (k=8 OLMoE, k=2 Mixtral; 1.5× → 12 experts on OLMoE) or score-based "all experts above a configurable percentile threshold (the default is 80th percentile)" (lines 314-322); "we resort to prefetching only the very next layer experts" (lines 309-310).
- Motivation: "MoE expert access follows a deterministic front-to-back layer sequence (layer 0→1→2→· · · →15) within each forward pass. Once we move on from a layer, that layer's experts will not be needed until the next forward pass" (lines 368-370); LRU/LFU/FLD/SB all make "experts from future layers get evicted prematurely" (lines 374-375). Collision miss = "a type of cache miss where experts are evicted and then immediately needed again in the same forward pass" (lines 383-384).
- Staleness: "An expert is considered stale if it was accessed in a previous forward cycle; otherwise, it is current, meaning it is used in the current pass or selected by prefetching. We describe an expert as left if it appears earlier than the current execution point in layer–expert order" (lines 387-389).
- Rule: cache "is partitioned into a stale queue containing experts from previous forward passes and a current queue containing experts from the ongoing pass" (lines 390-392). "Eviction prioritizes stale experts ... Experts in the current queue are evicted only to prevent out-of-memory errors. Within each queue, experts follow FIFO ordering based on layer position" (lines 393-396), i.e. LS "only evict[s] stale left-layer experts" (lines 470, 865). Priority heaps: O(N) space, O(log N) insert, O(1) evict (lines 397-398). No pseudo-code is given for LS itself.
- With prefetching: a prefetched expert counts as current (line 388) and is protected "to prevent premature eviction of prefetched future experts"; stale experts "are ... prioritized for eviction, unless they are needed again via prefetching" (lines 450-454; also 792-794).

## 5. Expert substitution and fetch cascade
- Substitution (Zhu et al. 2025) = "replacing cache-miss experts with functionally similar cached experts whose scores fall within tolerance thresholds" (lines 139-141; "based on score proximity", lines 258-259). Verdict: "quality degrades significantly in practice" (line 259); "Substitution policies fall outside the chart range" of Fig. 7 (line 527); "Substitution Score fails across all configurations, suggesting that replacing missing experts with functionally similar alternatives is unreliable, regardless of capacity level" (lines 583-585).
- Numbers (Table 1, Config#2 vs Config#1): GSM8K OLMoE 0.073 vs 0.677, Mixtral 0.037 vs 0.566, Phi 0.027 vs 0.705, Qwen 0.008 vs 0.506 — i.e. GSM8K goes to near zero on all four models. TruthfulQA 0.493/0.251/0.514/0.480 vs 0.555/0.343/0.775/0.614; NQ 0.159/0.273/0.269/0.408 vs 0.343/0.518/0.589/0.473 (roughly halved on three models). Caveat: Config#2 also swaps in score-based prefetch and score-based eviction, so Table 1 does not isolate substitution; the isolated evidence is Fig. 7 (off-chart, no numbers).
- Fetch cascade (HOBBIT: "int4 for float16, int2 for int8" on a miss, lines 138-139; Config#3 Spec+LHU+Cascade): lowest fetch-based TTFT (OLMoE 1590 vs 3077 for #1) but GSM8K 0.606/0.310/0.604/0.217 vs 0.677/0.566/0.705/0.506 and lower Token/s on every model (1.34/0.71/1.05/2.19 vs 1.91/0.98/1.69/2.89). LS as drop-in on HOBBIT: 17.7% / 25.7% / 44.1% / 18.4% TTFT reduction (Table 2). The gentler Fetch-priority variant (always highest precision, fallback int8→int4→int2) is the Pareto winner of §5.4.

## 7. Answers
- (a) ~50% capacity: not tested — "We software-limit our GPU cache to 1%, 5%, or 25% of full model size" (lines 432-433). Decode phase: the only decode metric is Token/s in Table 1 at 5%; there the LS config (#5) is slower than the LRU configs on three of four models (OLMoE 1.69 vs 1.91/2.33, Phi 1.11 vs 1.69/1.72, Qwen 2.11 vs 2.89/3.50) and faster only on Mixtral (1.42 vs 0.98/1.07) — and those configs differ in prefetch too. Every LS-vs-LRU drop-in gain is TTFT (prefill): "Least-Stale achieves 10.7-34.7% TTFT improvements on OLMoE" (line 474), "an average 17% improvement in TTFT" (line 628). So: no decode-phase win over LRU is demonstrated, and nothing at 50%.
- (b) Per-layer vs global cache: not stated as a comparison. The framework uses one global budget ("GPU cache to 1%, 5%, or 25% of full model size", line 432); prior LRU "keeping a fixed number of recently-used experts across all layers" (lines 133-134); LFU "prioritizes retaining frequently-accessed experts across all layers" (line 812). LS keeps layer awareness inside that single cache ("FIFO ordering based on layer position", lines 395-396). Per-layer adaptivity appears only in prefetch count: "Giving each layer the ability to adapt the number of experts to prefetch based on gating scores" (lines 484-486).
