## 2. TLNLE analysis ("Top Layers Need Less Experts", Sec. 3.2, Fig. 1, Tab. 8)
- What was reduced: the top-k gate size per layer, Eq. 4: Expert_N(k,l,r) = k-1 for l <= L <= r, else k (l.211-214). Done only on Mixtral-8x7B (8 experts, top-2, 32 layers) with k fixed at 2, so layers in the swept range run top-1 and all others top-2 (l.189-192, l.219). Hypothesis: "activating fewer experts in deeper layers within a layer range [l, r] has a limited impact" (l.203-206).
- Granularity: 32 layers in 8 groups of 4; reduced range = [4*gidx_s, 4*gidx_e+3] (l.186-192). Metric: average zero-shot accuracy on the 8 general tasks of Sec. 4.1 (l.193-195, l.216-218). Fig. 1 is a gidx_s x gidx_e heat map of that average, colour bar 0.65-0.68 (l.131-184); the numbers are Tab. 8 (l.902-940), which also prints Act (= average experts per token).
- Stated findings: "post-trained MoE models usually can activate fewer experts in their upper layers" (l.87-89); "when l is 16 and r is 31, which means layers [16, 31] activate only the top-1 expert, while retaining top-2 experts for layers from 0 to 15, we observe only a 1.1% decrease in performance" (l.224-227); "TLNLE identifies layers, typically near the middle, that are most sensitive to expert count and are therefore chosen for hook insertion" (l.115-117).

Drops: 4-7 -1.01 > 12-15 -0.84 > 0-3 -0.82 > 16-19 -0.68 > 8-11 -0.66 > 20-23 -0.49 > 24-27 -0.44 > 28-31 -0.14.

## 3. Hook module (Sec. 3.3, Fig. 2)
- Two linear layers with ReLU as a scaled residual: x = x + Linear(ReLU(Linear(x))) * r, r trainable, initialised to 0.5 (Eq. 6, l.289-292). Its output is the router input, Eq. 1 (l.296-297).
- Inserted in ONE transformer block: "before the Feed-Forward layer in a single transformer block" (l.101-103), "in front of the gate within the MoE module" (l.437-438), after the post-attention Add&Norm (Fig. 2b, l.231-253).
- Hidden size = expert intermediate size for coarse-grained MoEs (Mixtral), twice that for fine-grained (Qwen, DeepSeek) (l.292-296, l.382-390). Trainable share "0.14% to 0.70%" of all parameters (l.76, l.395); Tab. 3 total-parameter deltas are +0.12B, +0.20B, +0.01B, +0.01B (l.448-462).
- Layer choice (l.294-300, l.261-277): fix l, lower k until the drop exceeds p or k = 2; then lower l with the same criterion; finally set ALL layers to k-1 and insert the Hook at layer l to compensate for the extra cut below l. p "typically ... 1.0%" (l.259) but Sec. 4.2 says tolerance "r to 1.5%", found by "a binary search procedure" (l.432-436). For Mixtral-8x7B Tab. 5's TLNLE (2,2) column is 2.00 in layers 0-1 and 1.00 in layers 2-31, i.e. l = 2 (l.498-500, l.825-865); Hook layers for the other models: not stated.
- Training: 512 calibration samples (C4 for general tasks, MATH for math), sequence length 2048, lr 1e-4, 1000 epochs, everything else frozen (l.374-399); "each baseline model can complete its training in four hours when using 8 H800 GPUs" (l.78-80).
- Why it compensates: state-space argument, Eq. 7-10 -- minimise ||S_H - S_v|| on a calibration set whose distribution matches the evaluation data (l.325-362). Calibration domain matters: Tab. 2 (see Sec. 5).

## 4. Setup (Sec. 4.1, Tab. 1)
- Models (Tab. 1 l.305-323, Tab. 3 l.444-462): Mixtral-8x7B 2/8 experts, 0 shared, GQA, 46.70B; Mixtral-8x22B 2/8, 0 shared, GQA, 140.60B; Qwen1.5-14.3B-A2.7B printed "8 / 64" (Activ./Total) with 4 shared experts, d_expert = 1/4 d_ffn, MHA, 14.31B, Tab. 3 counts routed k=4; DeepSeek-V2-Lite printed "8 / 66" with 2 shared, d_expert ~ 1/8 d_ffn, MLA, 16.40B, Tab. 3 routed k=6. Parameter range 14.3B-140.6B (l.124-125).
- Hardware: Hook training 8x H800 (l.80). Speed: 1 node 2x NVIDIA A800-SXM-80GB (400GB/s NVLink, BF16 312 TFLOPS) and 1 node 2x NVIDIA H20-SXM-96GB (900GB/s NVLink, 148 TFLOPS), BF16, HF transformers (l.566-586); SGLang v0.4.6.post5 with pipeline parallelism in A.7 (l.871-883). ## 6. Limitations (stated, l.597-620) and gaps relevant to local decode speed
- Stated: (1) "the hook method demonstrates stronger compensation when fewer experts are activated. However, as the number of activated experts increases and approaches the original setting, the accuracy compensation may become less significant" (l.606-612); (2) "we have not evaluated MoEs with over 141B parameters, such as DeepSeek-V3, Grok-1 and Qwen3" (l.612-615).
- Fine-grained MoEs: only Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B (routed top-4 -> top-1) and DeepSeek-V2-Lite (top-6 -> top-3); Hook recovers just +0.31 / +0.32 there. Authors: their experts are "less capable and there is greater redundancy among them ... limiting the potential for compensation" (l.486-495). No model with routed top-8 over 64 experts is tested; the per-layer TLNLE sweep and every dynamic-k comparison are Mixtral-8x7B only (l.219, l.504-505).
- Decode: ITL gain is 1.09x-1.13x (Transformers) / 1.21x-1.23x (SGLang) against 1.24x-1.99x for prefill; the authors attribute the gap to CPU scheduling overhead, "up to 50% of the total inference time" per Nsight profiling (l.567-578). Greedy decoding: not stated. Batch size: not stated. No offload or memory-bound setting is measured (l.38-42).
- Math is not recovered (Tab. 2: 41.91 vs 48.34). Internal inconsistencies as printed: tolerance p = 1.0% (l.259) vs r = 1.5% (l.434); Act statistics over "approximately 1 billion tokens" (l.502-503) vs "2.1M tokens" (l.812-813); k=1 average 62.87 (Tab. 3) vs 64.42 (Tab. 8 row (0,7)); trainable share "0.14%–0.70%" vs Tab. 3 deltas of +0.01B on 14.31B and 16.40B.

## 7. Specific questions
- (a) Ranking: the top of the network tolerates the fewest experts; the lower-middle tolerates a cut least. Tab. 8 12-layer windows at top-1: 4-15 -2.30 (worst) > 12-23 -1.95 > 8-19 -1.92 > 0-11 -1.86 > 16-27 -1.28 > 20-31 -0.52 (best); halves: 0-15 -2.33 vs 16-31 -1.14; single groups: 4-7 -1.01 worst, 28-31 -0.14 best (Sec. 2). Prose: "layers [16, 31] activate only the top-1 expert ... we observe only a 1.1% decrease in performance" (l.225-227); but the paper also writes "TLNLE identifies layers, typically near the middle, that are most sensitive to expert count" (l.115-116).
- (b) 64+ experts with top-8: NOT IN TEXT. "The experiments were performed on Mixtral-8x7B." (l.219); "The results are based on the Mixtral-8x7B model." (l.504-505). The fine-grained models get only uniform top-k cuts (Tab. 3), their routed k is 4 and 6 (l.456, l.460), and Tab. 1's "8" activated includes the 4 / 2 shared experts.
- (c) Drop without Hook at the same k (Tab. 3): Mixtral-8x7B -4.93 (67.80 -> 62.87, l.448-449); Mixtral-8x22B -4.69 (70.22 -> 65.53, l.452-453); Qwen -2.18 (65.89 -> 63.71, l.456-457); DeepSeek-V2-Lite -2.13 (60.31 -> 58.18, l.460-461); math -6.91 (48.34 -> 41.43, Tab. 2 l.377-378). ## 8. Verification of secondary-source claims
- "about 2.5% average degradation, over 50% fewer activated experts, and a 1.42x prefill speed-up on four MoE models": CONFIRMED as the abstract's wording (l.36-42: "average performance degradation of only 2.5%", "more than 50%", "1.42× inference speed-up during the pre-fill stage"), with corrections: per-model with-Hook drops are 2.50 / 1.68 / 1.87 / 1.81 (mean 1.97; 2.5 is Mixtral-8x7B's); the expert cut is 50% for three models and 75% for Qwen ("50%–75%", l.81-82); the 1.42x was measured on Mixtral-8x7B only (l.560-561), TTFT on 2xH20 with Transformers at 1k tokens (Tab. 7), range 1.24x-1.46x, 1.99x with SGLang -- "on four MoE models" is WRONG for the speed-up.
- "the layers most sensitive to expert count sit typically near the middle": CONFIRMED as the paper's own sentence (l.115-116). Tab. 8's numbers put the largest single-group drop at layers 4-7 and the worst 12-layer window at 4-15, with the lower half costing twice the upper half (Sec. 7a).
- "beats prior dynamic-k methods by over 0.6% at the same activated-expert count": CONFIRMED as stated -- intro: "by over 0.6% ... while reducing the same number of activated experts" (l.91-94); Sec. 4.3: "with a similar number of activated experts" (l.552-557). Precisely +0.64 over MoED (p=0.52, Act 1.07 vs 1.06) but only +0.56 over D2D (tau=0.9, Act 1.09), Tab. 4; single model (Mixtral-8x7B).
