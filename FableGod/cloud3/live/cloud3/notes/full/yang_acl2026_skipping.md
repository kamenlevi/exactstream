## 2. Static layer-wise top-k study (Sec. 4.2 and 5.4; Fig. 3, Fig. 5; Tables 9–10)
- Not a search over all schedules: "instead of enumerating all possible schedules, we construct a small family of interpretable shapes that shift the budget toward earlier, middle, or later layers" (l. 326–329). A schedule is a four-tuple (b, h, e, p): first layer gets b experts, layer p gets h, last layer gets e, every other layer by linear interpolation (l. 333–340). Shapes: ascending, descending, peak, valley (Fig. 3, l. 304). Quality is plotted against the average activated experts n̄a (Fig. 5); n̄a itself is not printed in the tables (a linear b→e ramp averages (b+e)/2).
- Grid run: DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Table 9 (l. 990–1164): b, h, e ∈ {2, 4, 6}, p ∈ {1, 6, 11, 16, 21, 26} = 162 rows. DeepSeek-V3, Table 10 (l. 1167–1221): 16 straight ramps (b, h, h, 61) with b, h ∈ {2, 4, 6, 8}, plus 36 rows with b, h, e ∈ {2, 5, 8} and p ∈ {10, 30, 50} = 52 rows. Fig. 5 also draws "Uniform", "Average" and "Range" series (l. 509–511).
- Finding (prose, l. 537–541): "the best-performing pattern differs between DeepSeek-V2-Lite and DeepSeek-V3: descending reductions work best for DeepSeek-V2-Lite, whereas ascending reductions work best for DeepSeek-V3". Per Fig. 3, ascending = expert count rises with depth (early layers get fewer experts); descending = late layers get fewer.
- V2-Lite numbers (Table 9; full model rows (6,6,6,·) = 65.9–66.0): descending (6,4,2,26) 66.1, (6,2,2,26) 65.2, (6,2,2,16) 64.6 vs the ascending mirrors (2,4,6,1) 65.0, (2,2,6,1) 64.0, (2,2,6,11) 62.0; uniform (4,4,4,·) 65.1–65.3, (2,2,2,·) 60.2–60.7.
- V3 numbers (Table 10; full (8,8,8,61) = 73.0): ascending ramps (2,8,8,61) 71.4, (4,8,8,61) 72.2, (6,8,8,61) 73.3, (2,6,6,61) 69.8, (2,4,4,61) 66.8 vs mirror descending ramps (8,2,2,61) 70.7, (8,4,4,61) 72.3, (8,6,6,61) 73.1, (6,2,2,61) 69.3, (4,2,2,61) 66.0; uniform (6,6,6,61) 72.9, (4,4,4,61) 70.6, (2,2,2,61) 60.8. Ascending wins at the small budgets and ties at the large ones; the uniform rows are at least as good as either ramp at equal (b+e)/2.
- Non-transfer is stated only qualitatively: "fails to transfer to DeepSeek-V3, and vice versa" (l. 58–60); "preferred static patterns vary across models and routing mechanisms, even reversing between DeepSeek-V2-Lite and DeepSeek-V3" (l. 569–572). Explanation offered: V3 "exhibits greater instability across different reduction strategies. This instability may stem from the inherent properties of the sigmoid function, where expert weights tend to polarize toward 0 or 1" (l. 516–521), versus softmax routing in V2-Lite. No transfer experiment (V2-Lite schedule applied to V3) is printed; the claim rests on the two grids.

## 3. ASET (Sec. 4.3, Eq. 4–9, Algorithm 1)
- Acts per token, per MoE layer, training-free, on p = softmax(r) sorted descending (l. 340–343); "fixed Top-k and fixed-threshold rules emerge as simpler non-adaptive special cases" (l. 110–112).
- Stage 1: candidate set C = smallest prefix with cumulative mass ≥ θcand (Eq. 4); "we set θcand = θmax in all experiments" (l. 368).
- Stage 2: renormalise the prefix (Eq. 5), Shannon entropy (Eq. 6), divide by log2(nw) → Hnorm ∈ [0, 1], Hnorm = 0 when nw = 1 (Eq. 7).
- Stage 3: θdyn = θmin + (θmax − θmin)·(Hnorm)^γ with γ > 1 (Eq. 8); S = Σ_{i≤na} p(i), the router's original Top-na mass; k_final = smallest k with Σ_{i≤k} p(i) ≥ S·θdyn (Eq. 9).
- Because θdyn ≤ 1, "we always have S·θdyn ≤ S, implying kfinal ≤ na ... never expanding the budget" (l. 366–369).
- Threshold values: numeric θmin, θmax, γ are not stated anywhere; each (θmin, θmax, γ) of a sweep is one point of Fig. 6; "k̄ is controlled mainly through (θmin, θmax), while γ plays a secondary shaping role" (l. 606–608).
- Calibration: only that sweep. OLMoE is used because "its router produces a normalized distribution that naturally supports entropy-based calibration" (l. 564–566); ASET is not run on the sigmoid-routed DeepSeek-V3 or on V2-Lite.
- Renormalisation of the surviving experts' routing weights after skipping: not stated (Eq. 5 renormalises only for the entropy).

## 4. Setup (Sec. 5.1–5.2, Appendix C–E)
- Models: DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat (ne = 64 per Table 1 baseline l. 827, na = 6 per Fig. 4a legend l. 420), DeepSeek-V3 (na = 8 per Fig. 4b legend l. 451; ne not printed per model), OLMoE-1B-7B-0125-Instruct (ne, na not printed). Generic statement: fine-grained MoE "selects 6 to 8 experts from a pool of 64 to 256" (l. 243–245).
- Hardware: V2-Lite serving on 2× NVIDIA Tesla A800 80G PCI-e (Table 5, l. 971); V3 on 8× NVIDIA Tesla H200 141G SXM5 (Table 6, l. 928); Sec. 3.2 microbenchmarks on 1× A800 (Table 4). OLMoE hardware: not stated.
- Software: "sglang build v0.4.4 post 1 (commit ad4e58bf67ec833ff4d036af5129ec6e1633efc4)" with sglang.bench (l. 978–980, 986–988); PyTorch + torch.compile, MixtralModel from Transformers, for Fig. 2 (l. 954–958).
- Throughput = "decoding throughput (generated tokens per second), excluding the prefill stage" (l. 390–392); "1024 randomly sampled input tokens and 1024 generated output tokens" (l. 414–416); concurrency = simultaneous in-flight requests. Table 2 shows the split matters: 256/1792 → 6368 vs 1792/256 → 13040 tok/s on V2-Lite; Table 3: V3 1024/1024 → 2636 vs 1024/8 → 8487.
- Quality: ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, BoolQ, OBQA, RTE, Winogrande; reported score is their average; "random-guess baseline of approximately 36" (l. 398–406).

## 5. Main results
- V2-Lite throughput, Fig. 4a / Table 7 (uniform na, baseline na = 6): "at low concurrency, the speedup ratio can reach 50% (when na = 2), exceeding the FLOPs-based compute-fraction bound (da/(ds + da) ≈ 43.6%; excluding shared components)" (l. 481–484). Table 7: conc 4: 729 → 1099 tok/s (+50.8% computed); conc 8: 1041 → 1581 (+51.9%); conc 128: 5591 → 6126 (+9.6%); conc 512: 9379 → 10954 (+16.8%); na = 5 at conc 512: 9783 (+4.3%). Gains are large at low and high concurrency and "limited at moderate concurrency" (l. 478–482).
- V3 throughput, Fig. 4b / Table 8 (baseline na = 8 → 6 / 4 / 3): conc 32: 1204 → 1331 / 1401 / 1705 (+10.5% / +16.4% / +41.6% computed); conc 128: 2751 → 2806 / 2985 / 3100 (+2.0% / +8.5% / +12.7%); conc 16: 769 → 793 / 820 / 979; conc 2: 163 → 164 / 167 / 170. Fig. 4b's speedup axis tops out at 1.4.
- V2-Lite accuracy, Fig. 5a (prose l. 497–509): "reducing the number of active experts from the full set to only two results in a performance drop of approximately 7.5%, while in the best-case scenario, the performance loss is limited to around 6%. Moreover, when an average of 3.3 experts is retained, the performance degradation remains within 1%." Table 9 range: 66.1 (6,4,2,26) down to 60.2 (2,2,2,21) against 66.0 full.
- V3 accuracy, Fig. 5b / Table 10: no prose numbers; 73.0 full, 72.9 uniform-6, 73.3 (6,8,8,61), 70.6 uniform-4, 60.8 uniform-2. The abstract's "≥10% improvement on DeepSeek-V3 without measurable loss" is not tied to a row anywhere in the text; the only pairing the tables support is na = 6: +10.5% at conc 32 (Table 8) with 72.9 vs 73.0 (Table 10).
- Pruning, Appendix A (Fig. 7, Tables 1 and 11, V2-Lite): cutting ne gives "up to 2.3× speedup" (l. 805–807; Table 11 conc 12: 1357 → 3190 for ne 64 → 6) but "With a 50% reduction in experts, even the best method experienced a 15% drop" (l. 795–797; Table 1 soft count ne = 32: 57.8 vs 66.0). Pruning + skipping at concurrency 6 (Fig. 9) "can boost throughput by as much as 78%" (l. 912–913): this combination is the source of the abstract's 78%.
- OLMoE / ASET, Fig. 6: no numbers printed and no table. Extracted axes: k̄ from 3 to 6, average score from 0.52 to 0.62, legend entries 1, 2, 3, 4 (unlabelled in the extraction). Prose: "As k̄ increases, the average score improves monotonically with diminishing returns" (l. 595–596); "points with different curvature parameters γ largely lie on the same frontier" (l. 602–604). No fixed-Top-k or static-schedule comparison point is printed for OLMoE.

## 6. Stated limitations (l. 601–625) and specific items
- Scope: fine-grained MoE only; "coarse-grained MoE offers much less budget headroom, and dense models do not expose an analogous activation control". Static layer-wise schedules "are not portable across routing mechanisms"; "we do not claim universal static schedules". "Evidence for ASET is therefore concentrated on OLMoE rather than all routing families".
- Metric: throughput "under controlled decoding"; "does not directly characterize deployment-level latency under realistic traffic, batching, or queueing conditions, or expert-parallel communication effects". Training-aware routing changes and finetuning are out of scope.
- Shared expert: always kept, never skipped; it appears only as ds (l. 192–193) and in the bound "excluding shared components" (l. 484). Greedy vs sampled decoding: not stated. Code tasks: none (only the six QA/NLI sets). Speculative decoding: cited once as an orthogonal direction, "speculative decoding under large-batch regimes (Shi et al., 2026)" (l. 160–163); never combined with skipping.

## 7. Specific questions
- (a) V3: early layers tolerate fewer experts. Quote: "whereas ascending reductions work best for DeepSeek-V3" (l. 540–541), ascending meaning na rises with depth (Fig. 3, l. 304). By how much: not stated in prose; Table 10 ramps ending at 8: first layer 2 → 71.4 (−1.6 vs 73.0), 4 → 72.2 (−0.8), 6 → 73.3 (+0.3); the middle-layer variants (2,5,8,10) 72.9 and (2,8,5,10) 72.7 also sit within 0.3 of full.
- (b) Uniform vs layer-wise at equal activation: no explicit prose number. Closest quote: "when an average of 3.3 experts is retained, the performance degradation remains within 1%" (l. 501–509, best layer-wise). From the tables at equal (b+e)/2: V2-Lite n̄a = 4: uniform 65.1–65.3 vs descending (6,2,2,26) 65.2 vs ascending (2,2,6,1) 64.0; V3 n̄a = 4: uniform 70.6 vs (2,6,6,61) 69.8 vs (6,2,2,61) 69.3; V3 n̄a = 6: uniform 72.9 vs (4,8,8,61) 72.2 vs (8,4,4,61) 72.3. So uniform costs nothing extra against the straight ramps; the layer-wise gain is only against a wrong-direction ramp.
- (c) "Minimal degradation" numerically: V2-Lite "within 1%" at 3.3 average experts and "approximately 7.5%" at 2 (l. 497–509); V3 "without measurable loss" (l. 45, l. 624–625) corresponds at most to −0.1 point (72.9 vs 73.0, Table 10 uniform 6 vs 8). No seeds, variance or significance test are reported.

## 8. Verification of secondary-source claims
- "static skipping policies yield 10–78% throughput gains with minimal performance degradation, including ≥10% improvement on DeepSeek-V3 without measurable loss": CONFIRMED verbatim (abstract l. 41–45; conclusion l. 622–625 says "over 10%"). Caveat for anyone reusing it: the 78% is the pruning+skipping combination of Fig. 9 (l. 912–913), not skipping alone; skipping alone is printed as "50% (when na = 2)" (l. 482), Table 7 max +51.9% (computed).
- "the allocation strategy that works best for DeepSeek-V2-Lite fails to transfer to DeepSeek-V3, and vice versa": CONFIRMED verbatim (l. 58–60); the directions are descending for V2-Lite and ascending for V3 (l. 539–541).
