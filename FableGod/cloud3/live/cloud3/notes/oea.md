# OEA: Opportunistic Expert Activation, batch-aware expert routing for faster decode without retraining (arXiv 2511.02237, 4 Nov 2025; Oncescu, Wu, Chung, Wu, Gopal, Wang, Dao, Athiwaratkun; ICML 2026 poster)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02237 ; HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02237v1. Source: WebSearch extracts [S] (arXiv blocked here).
- What: two phases per decode batch: (1) each token keeps its top-k0 experts (a per-token quality floor); (2) "opportunistic piggybacking": each token adds experts already active in the batch (no new loads), capped by a maximum count. Additive (floor + already-loaded) vs Lynx's subtractive pruning of unpopular experts. Latency model: memory-bound roofline (time ~ unique experts).
- Numbers [S]: MoE-layer decode latency -39 % and -15 % (two settings; the project's NOVELTY.md reads them as Qwen3-30B-A3B / Qwen3-235B at batch 16) with no statistically significant accuracy loss; a GPQA-based latency average on Qwen3-30B-A3B in one summary.
- Maps to us: the union step of Q1-UNION / UNION-STACK is OEA ported to one 16-row check (NOVELTY.md:50). Measured here: OEA k8 (k0 8 on every row + union members) union x0.81-0.83 on all domains, PASS everywhere; sure 2 + unsure 8 code x0.527 / chat x0.76 (PASS code, BETWEEN chat); unsure 6 x0.435 / x0.60. OEA's cap on the max count is unused here (we add every union member at the model's own weight). The difference that matters: OEA keys k0 on nothing (uniform), we key it on the drafter's cf per row.
- TRIED status: ADOPTED-class (UNION-STACK PASS code; Q1U-T3; P1-OEA at 12 GB).
- Open: OEA's accuracy at k0 = 2-4 uniform on reasoning (they claim no significant loss at their settings; our map says topk2 on mid rows flips 26-44 %); whether their "already active" set includes experts loaded by other requests in the batch (it does: the batch is multi-request), which is why their union is larger and their cut per token bigger than ours at 16 rows of one chain.
