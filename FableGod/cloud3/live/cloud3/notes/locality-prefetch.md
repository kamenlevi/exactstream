# Routing locality and prefetch predictors (context for P2, not union levers): Local Routing Consistency (arXiv 2505.16056, Liang et al.), ST-MoE spatio-temporal prefetch (arXiv 2606.15453), ReMoE (2605.27081, ICML 2026), Temporal MoE (NeurIPS 2026), Oracle-MoE (ICML 2025), PreScope / LayerScope (2509.23638), PROBE (2602.00509), SpecMD (2602.03921)
- Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16056 (code github.com/ljcleo/moe-lrc) ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15453 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27081 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23638 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00509. Source: WebSearch extracts [S].
- LRC: two metrics, Segment Routing Best Performance (one fixed expert group serving a token segment) and Segment Cache Best Hit Rate (oracle cache over a segment); 20 MoE LLMs; models with MoE on every layer and NO shared expert have the highest local consistency (shared experts reduce it); a cache of ~2x the active experts balances hit rate and size; domain-specialised experts drive consistency more than vocabulary-specialised ones.
- ST-MoE: same-layer expert overlap across consecutive decoded tokens is ~2x a random-routing baseline across several LLMs / datasets; adjacent-layer correlation used for prefetch.
- ReMoE / StickyMoE / Temporal MoE / Oracle-MoE: TRAINING-time or router-fine-tuning locality (ReMoE +26 % expert reuse; StickyMoE switch rates up to -59 %; Temporal MoE 5.1x less serving memory at 0.70-0.83x speed): not applicable (no router retraining; TRIED STICKY35 KILL: trained sticky terms all worse than untrained on the 35B).
- PreScope (renamed LayerScope in a later version), PROBE: cross-layer schedulers and a gate-initialised lookahead predictor (~90 % next-layer hotspot accuracy): our V1 look-ahead / LEAD1CORR family (recall@10 0.61-0.73 here).
- Maps to us: these quantify the same thing our union curve measures: this model's consecutive rows share ~45 % of their experts (union 75 of 160 pairs at 16 rows), far above the random-routing baseline (MoESD's formula gives 139), and the share is what row-selective cuts live on. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next HAS a shared expert, which LRC says lowers consistency. Nothing here cuts distinct experts; LRC's SRP metric is a ready-made way to express "how well one expert group serves a 16-row block", which P2 computes directly.
