## 0. Verdict in five lines
1. **Attention is the only term of the check that grows with context, and it grows by compute and latency, not bytes [K R-81, P].** Per 16-row check the 12 QSA layers cost 5.8 ms at ~200 keys (C0), 12.6 at 2K, 16.1 at 4K, 22.7 at 8K, 24.3 at 10K, 36.9 at 16K, 37.2 at 20K: **~1.37 ms per 1K keys**. The KV cache they read is 24 KB per token (bf16, 2 kv heads x 256, 12 layers; +3 KB of indexer keys): 54 MB at 2K, 270 MB at 10K, 540 MB at 20K, i.e. 0.2 / 1.0 / 1.9 ms at 280 GB/s. The cost is the dense QK^T and PV over every key (9.4 GFLOP at 2K, 47 at 10K, 94 at 20K) run as 12 head-chunked, latency-bound calls of 5 kernels each at **0.7-2.5 TFLOPS** on a matrix unit that does ~28, with the indexer's selection applied as a mask afterwards (the selection itself costs ~0). GDN's 36 layers carry a fixed 3.1 MB state per layer and do not move.
2. **What it does to tok/s today [P on K]:** code (16 rows, kept 10.54): 61.4 tok/s at 200 keys -> 59.1 at 2K, 55.5 at 10K, 52 at 16-20K (-10 to -15 %); chat (6-row checks, kept 2.53; REF2 pads them to the 16-row kernels, so attention costs the same per check): 23.3 -> 21.9 at 2K, 19.9 at 10K, 18.1 at 16K (-15 to -22 %). At the floor engine the loss is larger because everything else shrank: code 163 -> 127 tok/s at 10K (-22 %), 110 at 16K (-33 %); chat 53 -> 38 (-28 %), 32 (-40 %). **Attention overtakes the experts per check at ~33K keys today (3c), ~28K with NAX6, ~16K at the floor engine; for chat at ~14K today and ~5.5K at the floor.** The other long-context cost is R-80's first check after a 9-12K prefill (1.6-3.4 s, the prefill's lazy tail or cold pools, not attention: its one-time kernel builds are tens of ms).
3. **EXACT fixes (bitwise = REF2-T) are capped [P on K]:** the bits are MLX's chunked SDPA with REF2b's 512-key buckets, and a key-split or any re-tiling changes the softmax and PV accumulation order. What keeps the bits: **X1** the one-kernel bitwise replica of the chunk set (BOUNDARIES E1's attention piece: -2 ms per check at any length, the 55 boundaries); **X2** skipping key tiles that every one of the 16 rows masks inside that replica (exact zeros added in MLX's order: R-81's paper pricing 1.3x at 10K, 2x at 20K on the SDPA part if the tile is 16 keys, ~1.1x if MLX's tile is 32) **-5 / -2.5 ms at 10K, -12 / -6 at 20K**; **X3** the 8-bit MMA for the indexer (D32-8bit, PARKED) -0.7 ms; the KV bytes and layout offer nothing (the bytes are 1 ms; #12b's lossless coding ~0 at 10K). EXACT attention stays ~18-20 ms at 10K and ~28 at 20K per check: the exact long-context wall is a slope of ~1.1 ms per K.
4. **FAST fixes [P on K, G]:** **F1 FATT16 with a key split** (R-66's kernel, batch- and N-invariant by construction, plus 4-8 simdgroups per head over absolute 32-key blocks with a fixed-order merge, blocks no row selects skipped): the latency-bound walk (one simdgroup per head over 312 blocks at 10K) becomes parallel: **24.3 -> ~8-10 ms per check at 10K (-14 / -7), 37 -> ~12-15 at 20K (-23 / -12)**; FAST-K, certified once by scores (REF3-T class, as att_single); its row-count variant (8 real rows for chat) halves chat's compute again (-4 / -2 at 10K). **F1b the compact union-list kernel** (4-key blocks gathered: the model's own 2,048-key selection computed and nothing else, 3.3x at 10K, 6x at 20K) is FAST-K but row-variant (a row's bits depend on its companions' selections), the same class as F2 chunked GDN: **-16 / -8 ms at 10K**. **F2 KV at 8 / 4 bits** (KIVI, KVQuant): bytes -50-75 % but bytes are 1-2 ms here: ~0 speed, a 12 GB memory option; **F3 windows on some layers** (StreamingLLM sinks + a 2K window on 8 of 12 layers): -8 / -4 at 10K, quality risk high untrained (long-range recall), redundant with the model's own indexer; **F4 KV eviction by the indexer's own selections** (H2O / SnapKV with the model's importance signal for free): -12 / -6 at 10K, quality risk on recall of old context in agent sessions. The one FAST fix with no model-semantics risk is F1: it computes exactly the model's attention, only in another order.
5. **The 10-minute screen for Leap (FAST-K, pre-registered in section 5):** FATT16 + a 4-way key split with a fixed-order merge + absolute 32-key block skipping, one real QSA layer at 16 rows, keys 2K / 10K / 20K, INV test as R-66 (rows alone == rows in the batch, masked keys added) and ms per call vs REF2-T's chunk set (R-81's numbers) and att_single. **PASS: >= 3x vs REF2-T at 10K (<= 0.68 ms per call = -16 ms per 16-row check at 10K) with INV PASS; BETWEEN 1.5-3x; KILL < 1.5x or INV FAIL.** Prediction: 2.5-4x (PASS 40 %, BETWEEN 50 %), INV PASS 85 %. The exact alternative (X2's tile skipping in the chunk-set replica, >= 1.3x at 10K) is the second 10 minutes if the first passes.

## 1. The check's cost against context length [K, P]
### 1.1 The engine's attention at a 16-row check (from the code and Leap's anatomy) [K]
QSA = gated GQA: 24 query heads, 2 kv heads, head_dim 256 (q_proj 12,288 x 2,560 4-bit on qmm16c 1.29 ms per check, k / v 512 x 2,560 0.60, o_proj's share 0.6 [K kern23]); RoPE; the KV cache in bf16 (`QSAKVCache` = mlx_lm's `KVCache`): 2 x 256 x 2 B x (K + V) = **2 KB per token per layer, 24 KB per token over the 12 layers**; the indexer: 4 query heads, 1 kv head, head_dim 128, pooled 4-token blocks, budget 2,048 keys = top-512 blocks per row, an early return at <= 2,048 keys (TRIED #16), its keys 256 B per token per layer (3 KB per token). At a 16-row check the verify runs MTPLX's head-chunked SDPA: q_len x GQA = 16 x 12 > 32, so 12 chunks (one per kv head and query-head group), each 5 kernels (scale, QK^T, mask select, softmax, PV) over a **dense mask** (the selected blocks + the causal tail) across every key, with REF2b's key buckets (keys padded to the next 512 from 512) so a row's bits do not depend on the exact key count within a bucket; 81-87 kernels per call at 383-1,100 keys with R-72's fused RoPE and indexer prep, 111 past ~2,048 keys [K r72_att_anat]. att_single (one SDPA call per layer, REF2c) is 2.1-2.8x faster and bit-identical only below 512 keys; FATT16 (R-66) is one fused kernel, batch- and N-invariant, more accurate than MLX against fp64, latency-bound (one simdgroup per head walks every 32-key block), PARKED.

## 2. EXACT fixes (bitwise = REF2-T) [P on K, G]
The bits are MLX's chunked SDPA inside REF2b's buckets. A key-split (flash-decoding), a different tile shape or a compacted key list all change the softmax's and PV's fp32 accumulation order, so they are not bitwise; what is:
| fix | mechanism | why bitwise | ms per 16-row check (bound / halved) | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **X1** the one-kernel replica of the chunk set | the 12 chunks x 5 kernels as one dispatch with MLX's tile order, softmax reduction and PV order verbatim; the mask build, RoPE and cache writes in its prologue / epilogue (BOUNDARIES E1) | the same operations in the same order; D1-B's K-320 replica is the precedent | -2 / -1 at every length (the boundaries and small kernels; the per-chunk matmuls still scale with keys) | in E1 |
| **X2** skip the key tiles that all 16 rows mask, inside X1 | a fully masked tile adds exact zeros to the softmax sum and PV accumulation in MLX's order, so leaving it out changes no bit; tiles stay absolute-position aligned | adding 0.0 is exact; -inf leaves the row max unchanged | if the tile is 16 keys: R-81's 1.3x at 10K / 2x at 20K on the SDPA part -> **-5 / -2.5 at 10K, -12 / -6 at 20K**; if MLX's tile is 32 keys, only ~6 % of tiles are empty at 10K: ~-1 / -0.5 [G] | not built; R-81 killed the FATT16 form on paper for the same reason |
| **X3** the 8-bit MMA kernel for the indexer and router matmuls (D32-8bit) | MLX runs them at 20-24 % of the byte floor | exact under its own numerics (a new kernel for an 8-bit shape; bits vs MLX to be checked) | -0.7 / -0.35 | PARKED |
| X4 KV bytes and layout as stored (bf16, 2 heads) | nothing to gain: 1 ms at 10K; #12b's lossless entropy coding ~0 at 10K (PARKED) | - | 0 | - |
| X5 chat's padded rows | REF2 pads 4-8-row chat checks to 16-row kernels; MLX's 16-row NAX tile holds the pads with the real rows, so no bitwise kernel can skip them | - | 0 on EXACT (the row-count variant lives in F1) | - |
**EXACT at long context, all in:** attention ~18-20 ms at 10K and ~28 at 20K per check (from 24.3 / 37.2): the slope stays ~1.1 ms per K. On the EXACT track long context is a wall that only fewer keys per check or the 32-row block (per token) moves.

## 3. FAST fixes (quality-defined; FAST-K = kernel numerics, FAST-Q = the model's attention changed) [P on K, G]
| fix | track | mechanism, prior art | ms per 16-row check at 10K (bound / halved) | quality risk, test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **F1 FATT16 + key split** | FAST-K, batch- and N-invariant by construction | R-66's fused flash-attention-2 kernel on the matrix units (one dispatch per call; INV PASS on all four forms; more accurate than MLX vs fp64) with its one latency-bound walk split over 4-8 simdgroups per head on absolute 32-key blocks and a fixed-order merge of the (m, l, O) partials (flash-decoding, Dao 2023; split-KV in FlashInfer); blocks that no row selects skipped (exact zeros, as X2); retires REF2b's key buckets and class splits | 24.3 -> ~8-10 ms: **-14 / -7** (20K: 37 -> ~12-15, **-23 / -12**); at 200 keys ~-2 (att_single's gain); the 8-row variant for chat (the per-row arithmetic is batch-independent, so computing only the real rows is invariant): **-4 / -2 more per chat check at 10K** | the model's attention computed exactly, in another order: rel error 1.8e-3 vs MLX's 2.3e-3 [K R-66]; REF3-T certification once (full-length SD == AR under the new kernel) + same scores; no semantics change |
| **F1b the union-list kernel** | FAST-K, **row-variant** (a row's tile positions depend on which blocks its companions selected, so its bits depend on the batch: SD != AR bitwise) | gather the union of the 16 rows' selected 4-key blocks (~30 % of keys at 10K, ~15 % at 20K [K R-81]) plus the causal tail into dense NAX tiles; compute only those: the model's own 2,048-key sparse attention and nothing else | SDPA x3.3 at 10K, x6 at 20K: **-16 / -8 at 10K, -30 / -15 at 20K** | the same arithmetic class as F2 chunked GDN (BOUNDARIES 4F): scores-certified; no semantics change |
| **F2 KV at 8 / 4 bits** | FAST-Q | KIVI (2-bit, per-channel keys / per-token values), KVQuant, FlexGen 4-bit; the engine's KV is bf16 | bytes -50-75 %, but bytes are 1 ms of 24 at 10K and 2 of 37 at 20K: **~0 speed** (the dequant adds work in a latency-bound kernel); memory 270 -> 68-135 MB at 10K | published losses ~0.1-0.5 ppl at 4-bit, larger at 2-bit without the KIVI layout; a 12 GB memory option, not a speed fix here |
| **F3 windows on some layers** | FAST-Q | StreamingLLM's attention sinks + a sliding window (Xiao et al. 2023) on k of the 12 layers; compute proportional to the window | 2K window on 8 of 12 layers: 24.3 -> ~16.5: **-8 / -4** | the model was not trained with windows; long-range recall drops (needle tests); redundant with the model's own indexer, which already limits each row to 2,048 selected keys: F1 / F1b get the sparse cost without changing what the model attends to |
| **F4 KV eviction by the indexer's selections** | FAST-Q | H2O (Zhang et al. 2023), SnapKV, Scissorhands, PyramidKV keep "heavy hitters"; here the indexer's selection counts over the last T checks are the importance signal for free; evict blocks never selected for T checks | keeps ~30-40 % of keys at 10K: **-12 / -6**; also -60 % of KV memory | an evicted block cannot be re-selected: recall of old context in agent sessions is the risk; needle-in-a-haystack and agent QA tests; the model's sparse design makes it less sensitive than a dense model [G] |
| F5 lossless KV coding | EXACT | #12b (PARKED): ~12 % of bytes at 100K, ~0 at 10K | 0 | - |

Reading: the only fix that attacks the measured cost (compute and latency in chunked kernels) without touching the model's attention is F1; F1b is its faster, row-variant twin; F2-F4 attack bytes or keys, and bytes are not the cost on this machine below ~50K keys. The FAST attention at 10K: ~8-10 ms per check (F1) or ~6-8 (F1b) against 24.3 today and ~19 EXACT.

## 4. What it changes in the project's numbers [P]
- **The published headline holds only to ~2K keys:** code tok/s at 10K is -10 % today and -22 % at the floor engine; chat -15 % / -28 %; at 16-20K, -15 to -33 % / -22 to -40 %. The dense constant that BOUNDARIES priced at ~34 ms of GPU (200 keys) is ~52 ms at 10K and ~65 at 16K on EXACT; with F1 ~38 / ~42 (FAST).
- **The 350 line:** the cloud's <= 17 ms dense pass was a 200-key number; at 10K EXACT adds ~13 ms, FAST (F1) ~3-4. The 32-row check's per-row attention term grows ~5x from 200 keys to 10K, so B3's "32 rows lose 14-30 %" (HEAD_MIXER_32) worsens with context.
- **Chat / agent sessions** are where long context bites: kept 2.5 per check at 16-row attention cost; F1's 8-row variant and the row-independent FATT16 are worth more there than on code.

## 5. The pre-registered 10-minute screen (Leap) [G]
- **Build:** R-66's `fatt16.py` with (a) a 4-way key split per head (each simdgroup walks a contiguous quarter of the absolute 32-key blocks and keeps (m, l, O)), merged in a fixed order (0 + 1 + 2 + 3 with the standard rescale), (b) absolute 32-key blocks skipped when the 16-row mask is all -inf for the block, (c) the real-row count as a parameter (16 and 8).
- **Test:** one real QSA layer (layer 3's weights, R-72's harness), 16-row check calls at 2,000 / 10,000 / 20,000 keys (and 8 rows at 10,000), 8 calls chained, median of 20; INV as R-66 (other rows' queries changed; the same query at another row; N grown by 37 / 300 masked keys; an AR-style call) must PASS at every length; ms per call against REF2-T's chunk set (R-81: 1.05 / 2.03 / 3.10) and att_single (0.83 / 1.71 / 2.91).
- **Lines:** PASS = INV PASS and >= 3x vs REF2-T at 10K (<= 0.68 ms per call, -16 ms per 16-row check at 10K); BETWEEN = 1.5-3x; KILL = < 1.5x or INV FAIL. The 8-row arm reports its own factor (prediction ~1.6-1.8x of the 16-row kernel's time).
- **Prediction:** 2.5-4x at 10K (R-66 was latency-bound at 1 simdgroup per head; the split is the fix it named), INV PASS 85 %; PASS 40 %, BETWEEN 50 %, KILL 10 %. If PASS, it joins the FAST bundle beside att_single's slot (REF3-T certification once), and X2's exact tile skipping in the chunk-set replica is the next 10 minutes for the EXACT track (line >= 1.3x at 10K).

## 6. TRIED and TABLE_350
R-81 ATT-CTX (PASS, the measurement) and the block-sparse FATT16 priced on paper (KILL under exactness: absolute groups only ~25 % empty at 10K): X2 keeps that verdict for EXACT and F1b is its row-variant FAST form, allowed since Kamen's two-track rule. R-62 att_single (BETWEEN, REF2c) and R-66 FATT16 (PARKED): F1 is FATT16 with the split R-66 itself named as the next lever. KV quantization, windows and eviction have no TRIED rows: F2-F4 are new here and priced as FAST-Q with their published risks; none of them beats F1 on this machine because the KV bytes are not the cost. Nothing killed is re-proposed under its old reason.
