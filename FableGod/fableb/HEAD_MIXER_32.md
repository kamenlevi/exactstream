## 0. Verdict in five lines
1. **#3 is exact, not new as a method, and real but small: it is a 1-row lever, not a 16-row one.** The bound is Cauchy-Schwarz with partial sums, FEXIPRO's method (SIGMOD 2017) seeded by the draft; it is exact with a one-ulp margin, ties included. On the 125B's code the target's logits are far more peaked than L1-33 and MoonShots' 2B proxy assumed (top-1 - top-2 median **12.0 logits** vs 0.93 on the proxy [K]); at that peakedness a progressive bound prunes **89 % of the vocabulary per row after 25 % of the dims and 99.99 % after 50 %** [P on K, synthetic flat h at the measured gap]. But a 16-row tile skips only what all 16 rows prune: the survivors' union leaves ~75-95 % of the bytes, so at 16 rows the head's 1.53 ms shrinks by **~0.2 ms (0.1 halved)**; at 1 row (1-row checks, chat's MTP chain) the bytes fall ~58 %: **-0.7 ms (-0.35 halved)** per head call. The decisive test is L1-33's: capture real final states (GPU 3-5 min), run `head_bound.py` on them (CPU).
2. **#4 is void on EXACT and unmeasured on FAST.** The "exact low-rank correction" is a full GEMM over the consumer's weights again (14.75 MB re-read; GP6: not cached), which costs more than the HC chain it hides: 0 ms [P on HC_READ]. The FAST form, predict-and-skip (the gates from the previous read, no down / up GEMMs), is worth **-8 ms bound, -4 halved** per check and is row-invariant, but its quality bar is HC8's (1.44 % of the map's rows, 0.00 % sure) against the only datum we have, the static-gates arm at 18.3 % / 3.7 % [K map]; a 2-minute capture and a CPU fit decide it, P(pass) ~15 % [G].
3. **32 rows cross over only through the SSD, not through the dense pass.** At equal SSD hiding the 32-row check costs x1.41-1.46 of the 16-row one at the floor engine on either track (x1.47-1.59 today), so it needs kept32 / kept16 >= 1.41-1.46; the copy override gives 1.163 on HumanEval+, 1.00 on MBPP, 1.004 on agent, DFlash3-XL 1.23: **32 rows lose by 14-30 % everywhere** [P on K]. The robust form is NEXT_DRAFTER's copy-driven block length: 32 rows inside long copies (where the override holds 95-99 % and the ratio is ~2.5), 16 elsewhere. MoonShots' M-18c replay (preliminary, 23:5x) anchors the FAST side: 32 + Q1 vs 16 + Q1 on code -1.3 % today / -0.7 % with NAX6 (deep-sure +4 %), i.e. the gate's depth-flat acceptance lifts the kept ratio to ~1.4-1.5, the cost factor, and no further.
4. **Verdicts:** #3 BETWEEN (a 2-hour test; PASS likely at 1 row, KILL likely at 16 rows); #4 EXACT KILL (arithmetic), FAST-Q BETWEEN (a 2-minute capture, low prior); 32 rows: KILL as a global switch on both tracks, PASS as the selective copy-driven block, and a pre-registered SSD-hiding measurement for the runahead build.

## 1. #3 The seeded exact argmax for the LM head
### 1.1 The bound, and whether it is exact in bf16 / fp32 arithmetic (ties included) [P]
The check needs, per row, whether the drafted token t is the argmax of s_v = h . w_v over the 248,320 rows (and the top-2 margin for cf and the relaxed rules). With s_t known (one row), any row whose upper bound U_v >= s_v falls below s_t cannot be the argmax. Three exact upper bounds, all from the same algebra:
- **global:** U_v = ||h|| ||w_v|| (Cauchy-Schwarz);
- **chunked:** U_v = sum_c ||h_c|| ||w_{v,c}|| over 40 chunks of 64 dims (tighter than global by Cauchy-Schwarz on the chunk norms; equal for flat vectors);
- **progressive:** after computing the exact partial sum over the first f of the dims, U_v = partial_v + sum_{c > f} ||h_c|| ||w_{v,c}|| (FEXIPRO's partial-product pruning);
- and the **code-box bound** from the 4-bit structure alone: per group g, max over codes in [0, 15] of scale x (h_g . code) + bias x sum(h_g) = scale x 15 x sum(max(h_i, 0)) (scale >= 0) or scale x 15 x sum(min(h_i, 0)) (scale < 0), plus bias x sum(h_g): it reads only the 40 MB of scales and biases (L1-33's "screen" at zero bits).
Exactness in the engine's arithmetic: the inequalities hold in real arithmetic; the engine's logit is the kernel's fp32 accumulation rounded to bf16. The fp32 dot of 2,560 terms of magnitude ~0.5 has absolute error under ~1e-4 [P], far below the bf16 ulp of a logit of 20-30 (0.125). So the rule "keep every row with U_v >= s_t - margin, margin = 1 bf16 ulp of s_t plus 1e-3" keeps every row whose kernel logit could equal or exceed t's, **ties included**: a row that ties t in bf16 has a true dot within one ulp, so its bound exceeds the threshold and it survives; the survivors are then computed by the same kernel in the same per-output K order (the sum over stages must be the kernel's order, or the survivors are simply recomputed in full: bitwise to REF2-T), and the argmax over {t} union survivors with the engine's tie rule (lowest index) equals the full argmax. For the top-2 (cf, R1 / R2) the threshold is the best known second score. **EXACT, with a margin; bitwise on the survivors' logits.**

### 1.2 What it prunes, on the real head [K] with a calibrated hidden state [P/G]
No capture of the trunk's final hidden state exists on disk (Leap's `l118_tgt` h is the drafter's context input, `hidden_norm(fc(taps))`; its logits reproduce nothing of the target's). What exists: the target's top-32 log-probs at every assistant position of the 24 HumanEval windows (n = 6,375) [K], and the head itself [K]. So the hidden state in the check is synthetic: flat (rms 1 per element, L1/L2 ~40, as every normed state measured here) with a planted cosine c to the candidate row; c is swept and read off where the synthetic gaps match the measured ones. Everything else (norms, chunk norms, spectrum, the pruning sets) is computed on the real rows.
- **The head** [K]: codes 317.8 MB + scales 19.9 + biases 19.9 = 357.6 MB. Row norms median 0.660, CV 0.13; only 0.03 % of rows are under half the median norm and 1.94 % under 0.75x: the global bound can never prune more than ~2 % unless the candidate's cosine exceeds 0.75. Chunk norms are flat (max / mean 1.21), so the 40-chunk bound equals the global one (0.996). The spectrum is flat: the top 1 / 10 / 100 / 256 / 640 / 1,280 of 2,560 dims hold 17.8 / 19.0 / 24.9 / 32.5 / 48.1 / 69.9 % of the energy, so FEXIPRO's SVD rotation concentrates little here (the 17.8 % in one direction is the rows' shared mean).
- **The target's peakedness on code** [K]: top-1 - top-2 median 12.0 logits (p10 4.18, p25 9.75, p75 13.5, p90 14.6); top-1 - top-32 median 18.1 (p10 14.3); p(top-1) median 1.000, p10 0.980. MoonShots' 2B proxy had a median gap of 0.93 (p10 0.13) and every bound-based screen kept all 248,320 rows [K screen_head.txt]; L1-33 priced the idea on that proxy. The 125B on code is a different regime.
- **The sweep** (`head_bound.out`, medians over 64 real candidate rows; fractions of the vocabulary pruned):

  The measured top-1 - top-32 gap (18.1) matches c = 0.9; the measured top-1 - top-2 (12.0) exceeds the synthetic 9.4 at c = 0.9, so the real states at the median position are at least this aligned (the random complement in the synthetic h is pessimistic: it overlaps the rows' shared mean). The p10 positions (gap 4.2) sit near c = 0.5. **So: the global and chunked bounds prune ~0-20 %, the code-box bound nothing, and only the progressive bound prunes, and it prunes almost everything once the first 25-50 % of the dims are summed exactly** [P on K].
- **Bytes per head evaluation, 1 row** [P], three stages (25 % of the codes and their scales for every row = 89 MB, then the next 25 % for the survivors, then the last 50 % for the survivors; plus a 20 MB norm table): c 0.9: 119 MB (33 %); c 0.7: 183 (51 %); c 0.5: 324 (91 %); c 0.3: 377 (105 %, a loss). Weighted by the measured gap distribution (~75 % of code positions at c >= 0.9, ~15 % near 0.7, ~10 % at or below 0.5): **~42 % of the bytes, -58 %**. The head at 1 row is ~1.3-1.5 ms (MLX's call; kern23's 16-row qmm16c 1.53, floor 1.28): **-0.7 ms per 1-row head call, -0.35 halved**, less three small stage kernels (~20 us).
- **16 rows** [P]: a 16-row tile skips a row only if all 16 rows prune it. Stage 1 is shared (89 MB); with every row at c 0.9 the stage-1 survivors' union is 1 - 0.891^16 = 84 % of the rows (75 MB of stage 2), the stage-2 union ~0.2 % (0.4 MB of stage 3): 184 MB, 52 %, -0.7 ms, but only if all 16 rows are that sure. With the 2-4 rows per tile that sit at the p10-p25 gaps (c ~0.5-0.7), their stage-2 survivors (29-71 %) dominate the union and stage 3 reads 50-90 % of the last half: 270-345 MB, 76-96 %, **-0.05 to -0.35 ms**. Per-row gemv passes over each row's own survivors are worse (16 x 11 % x 268 MB > the head). **Expected at 16 rows: -0.2 ms (-0.1 halved)**, ~0.2 % of the check.
- **The MTP head and the drafter's head** [P/G]: both are the trunk's lm_head (mtp.safetensors has no head; R-84: the drafter's head is the target's 4-bit lm_head). Chat's MTP chain evaluates the head one row per step with a seed (the chain's own next token), but the MTP's top-1 is less sure (chat depth-1 0.72-0.80 [K TrainLab]), so c ~0.5-0.7 and the bytes fall 10-50 %: -0.1 to -0.5 ms per MTP step. The drafter has no seed (it produces the candidates; a running-max bound is far weaker) and runs its head at 15-16 rows per block: ~0.

### 1.3 Prior art [K web]
- **FEXIPRO** (Li, Chan, Yiu, Mamoulis, SIGMOD 2017, https://www.comp.polyu.edu.hk/~csmlyiu/conf/SIGMOD17_fexipro.pdf): exact top-k inner-product retrieval by a sequential scan with pruning: an SVD transform so the first dims carry most of the product, partial-product (progressive) bounds, integer-approximation upper bounds, and a positivity reduction. The seeded progressive bound of #3 is this method with the draft as the initial best; the code-box bound is its integer bound. Earlier: LEMP (Teflioudi, Gemulla, 2015) and cone trees (Ram, Gray, 2012) for exact MIPS, all known to weaken as dimensions grow.
- **FR-Spec** (Zhao et al., ACL 2025, arXiv 2502.14856): restricts the drafter's vocabulary to frequent tokens, verification on the full vocabulary, -75 % LM-head compute for the drafter, 1.12x over EAGLE-2. Not exact for the target's head, orthogonal to #3 (it is this project's FR-Spec row in TRIED).
- **This project:** L1-33 (PARKED): a low-bit screen with a provable bound; MoonShots' 2B proxy found nothing pruned, and the test named there (capture real final states on 4 HumanEval windows, then a CPU screen) is still the right one. TRIED's "Certified-exact approximate greedy (interval arithmetic)" is a different bound (through the layers) and stays dead.
- **New here:** the seed (a strong candidate before any head work: FEXIPRO finds its best incrementally) and the measurement that the 125B's code logits are peaked enough (gap 12) for the progressive bound to bite per row, which the 2B proxy's 0.93 hid. Not new as a method.

## 2. #4 Predict-then-correct mixers
### 2.1 The arithmetic [P on HC_READ]
Per HC read, per row: normed = grouped RMS norm of the 4 streams (10,240); c = normed @ Wd^T (320); logits = normed @ Wi^T (4); mix1 = silu(c / 4); g = mix1 @ Wu^T (10,240); mixed = (1/4) sum_s sigmoid(g_s) (x) normed_s; inject = 2 sigmoid(logits / 4). Today a read costs 99 us (S4g) against 47 us of weight bytes, in a chain the next block's GEMM waits for [K D1-B]. Cloud's #4: predict g (or mixed) a layer early, launch the consumer GEMM on the prediction, correct with "an exact low-rank term".
- **The correction is not low-rank where it matters.** The consumer's output on the true input is W mixed^T; on the prediction W mixed_pred^T; the exact correction is W (mixed - mixed_pred)^T: a full GEMM over the same W (GDN's qkv 14.75 MB, the router + shared ~6 MB), whose bytes are read again (GP6: a GPU re-read right after costs the same, the SLC does not keep it [K]). Its cost (~70 us for qkv) is of the order of the chain it hides (99 us), and the chain is still there (the HC read must run to produce mixed for the write and the correction). Net <= 0. Inside the read, mix1 is 320 wide, but predicting it removes nothing: c still needs normed (the norm pass and the down GEMM), and the correction (mix1 - mix1_pred) @ Wu^T streams Wu again. **EXACT: void, 0 ms.** The only exact overlap of the HC chain is BOUNDARIES' E2 (independent kernels beside it), already priced.
- **FAST-Q, predict-and-skip:** take the gates and inject from the previous read (the same row's g_{L-1}, inject_{L-1}, or a per-layer linear map of them) and skip the down / up GEMMs: the read becomes the norm pass + the tail, ~15 us, so **-84 us x 96 = -8 ms per check bound, -4 halved** [P]. It is row-invariant (each row's prediction depends only on its own history), so SD == AR holds under the new reference; the quality is the whole question.
### 2.2 Prediction quality: what is known, what to measure [K / G]
No capture of per-layer HC coefficients exists (the map's arms ran on the GPU and kept flips, not gates). What the map measured [K HC_READ]: static gates (the mixer replaced by a constant) flip 18.3 % of the 10,316 rows and 3.7 % of the 5,437 sure rows; a plain mean of the raw streams flips 100 %; HC8 (8-bit mixers) flips 1.44 % and 0.00 % of sure rows and passed EvalSuite with a lean. A predictor from the previous layer sits between static and exact; it has to reach HC8's level to be FAST-passable [G]. The gates are a nonlinear function of the four streams after the block just wrote them, which is what the previous layer cannot see; P(pass) ~15 % [G].
**Test (Leap, then CPU):** capture g (10,240 fp32 or bf16) and inject (4) per read on ~1,000 code and chat tokens (2 MB per token, one 2-minute GPU run with hooks on leapk.hc_read); CPU: fit per-layer predictors (identity from the previous read; a per-channel linear map; a rank-64 map from mixed_{L-1}) on half, test on the other half, report the gate error as rel rms on the block input (HC8's is 3.0e-3 [K hc_algebra]); then the map harness with the predicted gates as an arm (like hc8w). **Lines:** PASS if the predicted-gate arm flips <= 1.5 % of rows and <= 0.1 % of sure rows (HC8's class) -> build the skip path (-4 ms halved); KILL if > 3 % of rows. **Row-invariant:** yes, by construction; new reference (FAST-Q), certified by scores.
