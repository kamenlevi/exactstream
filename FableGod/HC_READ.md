## 0. Verdict in five lines
1. **What it computes [K code]:** per layer twice (before the attention / GDN block, before the MoE block) and once at the end of the pass (97 reads per check), per row: the pending write `h += inject_s * block_out` (fused into the read); a grouped RMS norm of the 4 streams (`normed = bf16(bf16(h_s / rms_s) * w)`, w a 10,240-wide weight); `c = normed @ Wd^T` (10,240 -> 320); `logits = normed @ Wi^T` (10,240 -> 4); `mix1 = silu(c / 4)`; `g = mix1 @ Wu^T` (320 -> 10,240); the block input `mixed = (1/4) sum_s sigmoid(g_s) * normed_s` (2,560 wide); the write gate `inject = 2 sigmoid(logits / 4)` (one scalar per stream) for this block's output. Nothing is static: all three gates depend on the input through silu / sigmoid, so there are no static mixers to fold (HC static flips 18.3 % of the map's rows, 3.7 % of its sure rows; a plain mean of the raw streams flips 100 % [K map]); the only static parts are w and the two 1/4 scales. A read moves 13.21 MB of bf16 weights (Wd 6.55 + Wu 6.55 + Wi 0.08 + w 0.02) and ~2.3 MB of activations at DRAM level (the streams 16 x 10,240 bf16 = 328 KB, touched ~4 times, plus 3.6 MB of cache-level re-reads by the GEMM's 11 column tiles), 211 MFLOP. Per check: 1.281 GB of weights, ~0.22 GB of activations (mostly cache hits), 20.5 GFLOP [P]: byte-bound by construction, floor 4.36-4.97 ms at the measured 294-258 GB/s (4.5 at 284) in bf16; the mission's "~2.3 ms floor" is the 8-bit floor (0.641 GB), not a bf16 floor.
2. **Why 11.4 ms [P on K]:** the two GEMMs are not the problem any more. Leap's S4 merged GEMM (down + inject, N 324) standalone costs 2.29 ms per check = 23.6 us per read = 281 GB/s, at the floor [K A2]; MLX's up GEMM (K 320) 2.85 ms per check = 29 us per read = 223 GB/s, 80 % of the rate [K kern23]. Together 5.1 ms. The S4 chain measures 9.82 ms per 96 reads [K r09c] = 102 us per read, so **4.7 ms per check (49 us per read) is the dependent chain itself**: four small kernels (norm + pending write, partition sum, gate table, tail), each a dependent memory round trip of 3-10 us, five kernel boundaries at ~2.4 us each [K: E -> X removed 8 boundaries for 1.81 ms], and the two GEMMs' fill / drain inside a chain that never lets them overlap. The remaining ~1.5 ms between 9.8 and the C0 number is CPU graph build inside C0's synced per-piece records (8.5 ms per 16-row check over ~2,800 kernels, ~480 of them HC) [P/G]; unsynced it overlaps the GPU. Activation traffic is ~15 % of the bytes and mostly cache-resident; launches are cheap; occupancy of the merged GEMM was the wall until S4 (33 one-simdgroup tiles, loads 3 steps ahead) put it at the floor. So the read's cost is the **chain tax**, not bytes, not FLOPs, not occupancy.
3. **The structural remainder (the proof the mission asked for):** under REF2-T (MLX's own arithmetic, bitwise) the bf16 floor of 4.5 ms is reachable to within ~1.5-2 ms and no further. What cannot go, with the same bits: one true data dependency per read (the up GEMM needs the whole of c, i.e. the complete K walk of the merged GEMM: one kernel boundary plus one fill / drain, 6-9 us per read, 0.6-0.9 ms per check [P]), the rms before the first scaled A tile (a full pass over the streams precedes the K walk, 3-5 us per read, ~0.4 ms; removable only by computing the rms in the previous block's last kernel with MLX's reduction order), and the up GEMM's K-320 shape (160 tiles of ten k-steps: fill-dominated, ~25 vs 23 us) [G]. Everything else in the 4.7 ms is removable by fusing the small kernels into the GEMMs' prologues and epilogues, which is design 1. Below 4.5 ms only bytes can go: a lossless 12.16-bit pack of the bf16 mixers (floor 3.4, exact; BigMoE's 15d, bit-identical [K TRIED]) or HC8 (floor 2.3; quality-defined: 1.44 % of the map's rows flip, 0.00 % of sure rows, code 0.33 % [K map]).
4. **Three designs (ms per check of GPU; today's read ~9.9 GPU / 11.4 synced):** **D1 the two-kernel bitwise read** (REF2-T, no reference change): kernel A = pending write + grouped norm in the prologue of S4's merged GEMM; kernel B = partition sum + gate table in the prologue of a bitwise replica of MLX's K-320 GEMM over W_up with its columns interleaved by stream, and the tail (sigmoid table, product, the 4-stream sum in the reference's order, x 1/4) in its epilogue: floor 4.6, expected 6.5, bound 7.6 (-3.4 vs today; the K-B half alone, 5 -> 3 kernels, -2.6). **D2 the row-invariant one-pass read** (REF3-T): the norm weight folded into Wd / Wi, the merged GEMM on the raw streams with the per-stream 1 / rms applied per stream-aligned partition in the epilogue, the write and the rms in the previous block's last kernel: expected 6.0-6.2, only -0.3 to -0.6 beyond D1, and it moves the mixer's outputs by 55-64 % of HC8's deviation on the same inputs (rel rms 2.2e-3 on the block input vs 3.0e-3; 18 % of its bf16 bits vs 33 %) [K hc_algebra]: a reference change with a map test, not a free one. **D3 the lossless pack in D1's loaders** (exact): 1.281 -> 0.974 GB, floor 3.4, expected -0.8 to -1.1 on top of D1 if the bandwidth-bound walks absorb the decode (the pack died at 1-8 rows because that read was latency-bound: the reason is gone at 16 rows in D1). **D4 HC8 in D1's loaders** (quality-defined): floor 2.3, expected 3.4, bound 4.5. **D5** (exact-safe, small): the MLP write handed to the next layer's attention read as pending, as the attention write already is to the MLP read: -48 kernels, hc_w 0.7 -> ~0.2.
5. **The bet: D1, kernel B first** (the up GEMM replica with the gate prologue and the tail epilogue; Leap's own open item in R-82, "the tail could join the up matmul only with a bitwise replica of MLX's bf16 GEMM at K 320"), then kernel A, then D5; exact and bitwise, no reference change, -3.9 ms per check on today's ~32 ms dense constant (-2.8 at the bound); D3 next if its 10-minute screen holds (-1 ms, exact); D4 is Kamen's quality call (-2.5 ms more). The known-lever dense stack (22-27 ms realistic) becomes ~18-23 exact (~17-22 with D3, ~15-20 with HC8): the HC read is ~4 ms of the ~8 ms gap to the cloud's <= 17 ms line, and it does not close that line by itself (GDN 12.4 vs ~5 of floor and attention 7.3 are the rest). On the 350 stack's ~50-60 ms floor-engine cycle: +6-7 % tok/s exact, +12-13 % with HC8 [P on RELAX_350].

## 1. What the read computes (from the code)
`GatedResidual.__call__` (qwen4_exp.py 1294-1307) in the decode_verify phase, per row r (16 rows per check), streams s = 0..3, hidden d = 2,560, hc_hidden 10,240, low rank 320:
| step | expression | shapes | weights (bf16) | FLOPs per 16 rows | activations in -> out |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| write (pending, fused into the norm pass at verify widths) | `h_s += inject_s * block_out` | [16, 4, 2560] | - | 0.08 M | h 328 KB + block_out 82 KB -> h' 328 KB |
| grouped RMS norm | `rms_s = sqrt(mean(h_s^2) + 1e-6)`; `normed = bf16(bf16(h_s / rms_s) * w)` | w [10240] | 20 KB | 0.5 M | h' 328 KB -> normed 328 KB |
| down (+ inject, merged as N 324 since X5 / S4) | `c = normed @ Wd^T`, `logits = normed @ Wi^T` | Wd [320, 10240], Wi [4, 10240] | 6.55 MB + 82 KB | 106.2 M | normed (11 tiles x 328 KB, cache) -> fp32 partials 62 KB x 3 partitions |
| gate 1 | `mix1 = silu(c / 4)`, `inject = 2 sigmoid(logits / 4)` (tables of MLX's fused values at every bf16 input) | [16, 320], [16, 4] | - | - | 62 KB -> 10 KB |
| up | `g = mix1 @ Wu^T` | Wu [10240, 320] | 6.55 MB | 104.9 M | 10 KB -> g 328 KB |
| tail | `mixed = (1/4) sum_s bf16(sigmoid(g_s) * normed_s)`, streams summed from +0 in order, bf16 adds | [16, 2560] | - | 0.3 M | g 328 KB + normed 328 KB -> mixed 82 KB |
Totals per read: 13.21 MB of weights, 211 MFLOP, ~2.3 MB of DRAM-level activation traffic (plus 3.6 MB of cache-level re-reads of normed by the merged GEMM's 11 column tiles: 16 x 10,240 bf16 does not fit a threadgroup) [P from the shapes, hc_algebra.out section 1]. Per check: 96 reads with inject + the final mixer without it (13.13 MB) = 1.281 GB, 20.5 GFLOP, ~0.22 GB of activations. Static vs dynamic: Wd, Wu, Wi, w are static weights; every mixing coefficient (mix1, the 10,240 gates sigmoid(g), the 4 inject scalars) is a function of the current streams through a nonlinearity, so nothing mixes "statically" and no product of weights can be precomputed (silu sits between Wd and Wu; sigmoid after Wu). The read combine is over the **normed** streams, not the raw ones; the inject logits gate the **write**, not the read.
What runs today at 16 rows [K leapk.py, R-09b, R-82]: REF2's S4 read = `norm_p` (pending write + grouped norm with MLX's reduction order, one 256-thread threadgroup per (row, stream), 64 threadgroups) -> `s4_partials` (MLX's NAX split-K arithmetic replicated on 33 one-simdgroup tiles: 11 column tiles of 32 x 3 K partitions of 4,096 / 4,096 / 2,048, fp32 partials, weight loads 3 steps ahead) -> `_ACCUM` (the partition sum in MLX's order, cast to bf16) -> `gate1` (table lookups; `_ACCUM_GATE` fuses the two since R-82) -> MLX's own bf16 matmul for up -> `tail` (table lookup, bf16 product and the 4-stream sum in order). Six kernels per read (five with R-82), 576-480 per check. At 1 row the engine runs MTPLX's v3 read (gemv sums, a kernel-private 8-bit pack: not the 16-row arithmetic); at 2-8 rows MTPLX's three-dispatch hc_verify_read (gemv). REF2-T's bits at 16 rows are MLX's NAX split-K order, which is why 16 rows == 32 rows == a batch of two but != two 8-row calls [K r09b].

Reconciliation with Leap's measurements [K]: E eager 24.4 ms per 96 reads (14 kernels per read); X (6 kernels, MLX's matmuls) 22.6: fusing the elementwise ops is worth 1.8 ms = 2.4 us per removed boundary; X5 (down + inject as one N 324 GEMM) 14.7: the separate N 4 inject matmul cost ~8 ms because it walked K = 10,240 on one tile (kern23: 4.06 ms per check for 82 KB of weights); S4 9.82: MLX's split-K order on 33 one-simdgroup tiles with prefetch brought the merged GEMM from ~91 us (A2's MLX 8.85 ms) to 23.6 us; S8 (7 steps ahead) lost to register pressure; F4 / F8 (one simdgroup per tile over all partitions) lost occupancy. The knockouts in X5 (norm 3.0, merged GEMM 9.9, gate 10.0, up 4.0, tail 4.25, sum 31 against a 14.8 chain) are not additive because removing any stage lets the GPU overlap the two halves: the gate "costs" 10 ms with almost no work, which is the chain tax seen from the other side. bmm16 (a batch-invariant tensor-unit GEMM) beats S4 by 0.22 ms per check and changes bits: the GEMM is not the lever (A2 KILL). CA's in-check 31-34 ms at 1K / 8K was the eager chain (11 kernels per read) plus writes; MTPLX's 8-row read cost 13.2 ms in the same harness (gemv, 3 dispatches, 2 x the weight bytes at 16 rows). R-73's compiled write (-1 kernel) and R-82's fused gate (-1) are the chain tax being paid down one kernel at a time; the C0 numbers (Mon 11:04) are with K-73, before K-83.
Where the 49 us per read of chain go [P/G split of a [P] total]: five boundaries ~12 us (2.4 each); the norm kernel ~6-10 us (64 threadgroups, 410 KB in, 656 KB out, a reduction then a scale: two dependent memory round trips); the partition sum and the gate ~3-5 us each (62 KB and 10 KB of data: pure latency); the tail ~5-8 us (656 KB in, 82 KB out); the two GEMMs' fill / drain inside the chain ~10-15 us (S4's 33 simdgroups start their prefetch pipelines cold; the up GEMM's 160 tiles ramp after a sync). Activation bytes: the streams are read from DRAM once per read (the norm pass) and written once (the pending write), normed is written once and read by the tail once (DRAM level) and by the 11 column tiles (cache level), g is written and read once: ~4 DRAM touches of 328 KB per read, 0.22 GB per check, under 1 ms even if none hit the cache [P]. Small-matrix efficiency: the merged GEMM is at the floor (S4), the up GEMM at 80 % (K 320 is fill-dominated) [K]. Launches: 480-576 per check at ~2.4 us of boundary each = 1.2-1.4 ms, part of the 4.7 [P].

Not viable, with the reason [P/K]: **merging the attention-side and MLP-side reads**: `(h + inject (x) o) @ W^T = h @ W^T + sum_s inject_s (o @ W_s^T)` holds (hc_algebra.out section 6, 7e-15), but the right side still reads every one of the four 2,560-wide weight slices (all 6.6 MB) and the new rms is nonlinear in o; the two reads are separated by the block, so there is no overlap to win either. **Precomputing the low-rank product Wd x Wu**: silu(c / 4) sits between them, so no product exists; without the nonlinearity it would be 10,240 x 10,240 = 210 MB per read, 16 x the bytes. **Folding static mixers into neighbouring weights**: there are none (section 1); w folds into Wd / Wi only under a new reference (D2) and saves no bytes. **Reading each stream once per layer**: the streams are already read from DRAM once per read; the two reads of a layer see different streams (the attention write lies between them); the 11 column-tile re-reads are cache-level (3.6 MB per read). **A layout change of the streams**: activations are ~15 % of the bytes and mostly cache hits; the layout change that pays is on W_up's columns (D1's kernel B). **Mixing in the previous layer's matmul epilogues**: the write and the rms can ride there (D2, D5); the mixing itself is the two GEMMs over 13 MB of weights, which no epilogue can absorb, and the merged GEMM cannot start before the whole written row exists. **HC4** (map: 8.04 % of rows flip, code 1.72 %; KILL), **HC static** (18.3 % / 3.72 % of sure rows; KILL), **the raw mean** (100 %), **2-bit HC** (killed earlier) are quality kills on the weights or the structure; D1, D3, D5 keep the bf16 weights and the arithmetic.

## 4. What it does to the dense pass and to 350 [P on K]
Today's dense constant is ~32 ms of GPU per 16-row check (C0: GDN 12.4, HC reads 11.4 synced / ~9.9 GPU, attention 7.3, router + shared + tail 5.1, head 2.7, rest 1.5). D1 + D5: HC ~6.5 GPU (-3.4) and hc_w -0.5 -> ~28 ms; + D3 ~27; + HC8 (D4) ~24.5. The cloud's realistic known-lever stack (22-27 ms: GDN one-kernel body, attention and tail fusions) becomes ~18-23 exact (D1 + D5), ~17-22 with D3, ~15-20 with HC8. The <= 17 ms line needs the stack's optimistic end plus D3, or HC8: the HC read is ~4 ms of the ~8 ms gap, and GDN (12.4 vs ~5 of floor) and attention are the rest. On the 350 stack (RELAX_350 / NEXT_DRAFTER: the floor engine at the realizable gate, a ~50-60 ms cycle at kept ~12.5-13): -3.9 ms is +6-7 % tok/s exact; -6.4 with HC8 is +12-13 %. At 12 GB the HC read is 3.2 % of decode (TRIED R-14): nothing there.

## 5. The CPU check (hc_algebra.py -> hc_algebra.out, 0.6 s, numpy, random weights at the model's shapes) [K]
1. D1's restructured arithmetic equals the reference chain bitwise on c, inject, g and mixed (0.00 % of bits differ): the column interleave and the prologue / epilogue fusions are algebraically free; the open questions are MLX's kernel properties (section 3, D1's checks), not the algebra.
2. A split-K order change alone (16 even partitions instead of 3, fp32 partials) touches 0.02 % of c's bf16 bits and 0.07 % of the block input's, all within 1 ulp: fp32 reordering is 100 x below the bf16 grain.
3. D2 (fold + raw streams + epilogue scales) moves c by rel rms 3.6e-3 (55 % of bits, 79 % within 1 ulp) and the block input by 2.2e-3 (18 %, 93 % within 1 ulp); with the fold kept in fp32, 3.2e-3 / 2.0e-3: the deviation is the reference's own two bf16 roundings of normed that D2 skips, i.e. bf16's grain, not an error. HC8 (8-bit g64 affine RTN, the reference arithmetic) on the same inputs: 5.8e-3 / 3.0e-3 (71 % / 33 % of bits). Ratio HC8 / D2: 1.4-1.8 x. So D2 is a reference change of HC8's order, and the map test HC8 took (flips on sure rows) is the right test for it.
4. D2 is row-invariant by construction (rows 0 / 7 / 15 alone == inside the 16-row batch, every output bitwise), which is REF3-T's requirement; the GPU kernel inherits it only if its per-output order is fixed independent of M (the screen's 1-row == 16-row bits).
5. The merge identity (section 3) holds to 7e-15.
Emulation caveat: the GEMMs are explicit fixed-order fp32 accumulations and the tail's 4-stream sum is fp32; leapk's tail adds in bf16. Both sides of each comparison use the same emulation, so the conclusions (bitwise equality of D1, the size of D2's and HC8's deviations) do not depend on it.

## 6. The structural remainder, stated once
With REF2-T's bits: per read, at least two kernels (the up GEMM needs the complete c), hence >= 1 boundary + 2 fills (~6-9 us), the rms pass before the first scaled tile (~3-5 us), and the up GEMM's K-320 fill (~2 us over its 23 us floor): ~12-16 us per read over the 46 us byte floor, i.e. 5.8-6.1 ms per check against 4.5 [P/G]. D1 reaches ~6.5 by this arithmetic, D2 ~6.0 by removing the rms pass at the price of the reference. The floor itself (4.5 bf16) is not a wall: D3 moves it to 3.4 exactly, D4 to 2.3 with a lean. The mission's "11.4 vs 2.3" is therefore three different things: 1.5 ms of CPU build that overlaps when unsynced, 4.7 ms of chain tax (removable, bitwise), and a 2.2 ms gap between the bf16 and 8-bit floors (a quality decision).
