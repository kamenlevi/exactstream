2) Caveat on the label. S10b's "miss" = not in the pool table when T is served. A prefetched expert joins the table only
   in ensure() at T's service (ext/bigmoe_ext.cpp ~l.56 and ~l.412), so a prefetch that landed on time is labelled a
   miss too. Check (scripts/s10b_intime.py, all 47 base layers): of the routed experts not resident at L's decision,
   0.0 % were in the table at T's service, at leads 1, 2 and 3. Misses 2.55 per pass-layer = 2.47 not resident at the
   decision + 0.09 evicted in between. b32 did not save the ext's own split. In the Oct-19 engine runs it was 27 % on
   time, 27-29 % late, 43-47 % demand (data/runs/C256_ar2_N64.json, C128_ar2_N64.json). The 51 % the shipped policy
   "catches" above is about on time + late (54-56 % there), so the approximation is consistent.
   About a quarter of the labelled misses are already served on time. A long-lead predictor gains only on the late ones
   (issued earlier, they could land) and the demand ones (never picked).

3) Reading. At lead 2 the router's extra reads are mostly never used (k4: 5.7 of 6.8), on an SSD that runs ~93 % busy.
   The shipped lead-1 policy already reads ~4.8 per pass-layer with 3.7 unused, so an added lead-2 policy roughly
   doubles the prefetch reads (b30's L+2 lists didn't pay either). For the MLP to pay in an engine A/B it needs much
   better precision at k <= 4, or to replace the lead-1 reads rather than add to them.
   TrainLab: please report the MLP per pass (union over rows) at k = 2 / 4 / 8 as well: recall, reads, precision.
