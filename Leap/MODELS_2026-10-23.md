# Is there a better model than Qwen3.8-Flash-Next for small memory and high speed on Kamen's Mac?
Leap, Fri 2026-10-23 15:25. Kamen's question, asked via the orchestrator.

**Answer: no.**
- Flash-Next is on the intelligence-vs-speed frontier at every memory size (12 GB, 24 GB, in RAM), by a wide margin.
- Every model that is smarter (AA 41-46) has 16-104B active parameters per token and is 180 GB to 1.6 TB at 4-bit.
  - None of them even fits the free disk: 121 GB free, with a floor of 100-110 GB.
  - Streamed at 24 GB they would run at about 1-6 tok/s.
- The faster models are much less intelligent: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (AA 18), Nemotron 3.5 Bolt (21).
- Two useful finds:
  - (1) A NATIVE DFlash2 drafter for Flash-Next exists (incoai, released Oct 19).
  - (2) Qwen3.8-27B (dense, Apache-2.0, 16 GB) is the simplest option for a 16-24 GB Mac without streaming, at a
    lower AA score (33.7 vs 39.8).

## Ranking: intelligence at a given speed
- **12 GB.**
  - Flash-Next's B12 is the only option near AA 40: 26-37 tok/s, non-exact, Eval suite verdict borderline (4/5).
  - Qwen3.6-35B-A3B is ~3x faster (~100 tok/s) at less than half the score (18).
  - Bolt: 18-28 tok/s at 21.
  - Everything above AA 40 runs at 2-5 tok/s.
- **24 GB.**
  - Flash-Next: 30-41 tok/s exact / v3, 49 in the opt-in fast mode, at 39.8.
  - Ahead of Qwen3.8-27B (~30 at 33.7) and Bolt (27-42 at 21).
  - GLM-5.3-Flash (42) and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 (45) give +2-5 points at ~1/7 of the speed, and do not fit the disk.
- **In RAM.**
  - Flash-Next v3: ~65 tok/s at ~40.
  - Qwen3.6-35B-A3B: 103 tok/s at 18.
  - Bolt: ~55 tok/s at 21.
- So no listed model beats Flash-Next on both axes anywhere. Only lower-intelligence models are faster.

## For the research line (cc NewIdeas)
- **incoai/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-DFlash2** is a native one-pass block drafter for OUR model. Not known in our notes;
  Chat1000 retargeted another DFlash. Its specs:
  - 5 layers at hidden 2560, taps of target layers 5/15/25/35/45, block 8 (so up to 7 drafts per pass), top-16
    candidates per position plus a path selector. Apache-2.0, ships a feature-capture patch for SGLang.
  - Card numbers: SGLang on a GB300, T=1 sampling, block 4 only, because their sparse-attention verify is limited.
    | task | MTP | DFlash2 | gain |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | HumanEval | 2.52 | 2.94 | +17 % |
    | MT-Bench | 2.50 | 2.83 | +13 % |
    | GSM8K | 3.08 | 3.26 | +6 % |
  - Single-stream speedup over plain decoding: 1.63-1.82x for DFlash2 vs 1.35-1.59x for MTP.
  - Published, untested here. It is the obvious first drafter for the drafter program: tau8 on code / agentic in our
    engine. Its top-16 candidates per position also feed the fork-tree idea (A1 result).
- None of the alternatives changes the 350 picture: they are either less intelligent or far bigger per token.

## Method (scripts/model_speed.py, model_table.py; calibrated on our own measurements)
- **Plain decode in RAM** = bytes read per token / (0.55 x 280 GB/s).
  - Calibration: the 35B-A3B measured 75-83 tok/s at 2.00 GB/token.
  - Dense models use 0.75 x 280 GB/s (large GEMVs).
- **Streamed at a budget.**
  - Dense, non-expert weights stay resident (budget minus 1.5 GB); the rest caches experts.
  - Expert misses per token = active experts x (1 - hit rate), each read from the SSD at 13 GB/s.
  - Hit rate vs cache fraction comes from our 125B traces: 0.956 at 50 %, ~0.80 at 25 %, extrapolated below.
- **Self-check on the 125B:** 26 / 41 tok/s at 34 GB (measured 25-27 / 41-42); 21 / 33 at 24 GB (measured 22-30).
- **Drafter multipliers:** x1.9 in RAM (125B MTP), x1.55 streamed (measured), x2.5 for a dense target with MTP /
  DFlash2.
- **Caveats.**
  - Other models' routing locality may differ from the 125B's. Coarse experts (14-57 MB) make each miss costlier.
  - Estimates are +-30 %.
  - The ranking is robust: the large models lose by 7-30x.
- **Disk:** 121 GB free now, with a floor of ~100-110 GB. Any download beyond ~10-20 GB means deleting something first.
